## S1 and S2: protocol

Same protocol as the v0.14.2 view study, with the client count as a parameter. Each client holds 8
distinct public digits and takes one full-batch SGD step from a shared initial model (learning rate 0.15).
Client 0 is the victim. The attacker knows the labels, the learning rate and the initial model.
Per-image MSE below 0.05 is "recognisable".

- `plaintext_individual`: the victim update.
- `secagg_aggregate`: only the SecAgg+ aggregate. The attacker holds the labels of all contributors (joint oracle, as in v0.14.2) and
  matches 8 x clients images at once. The aggregate comes from the upstream quantiser (clipping range 8, quantisation range 2^22).
- `secagg_collusion_n-1`: the aggregate minus the other clients' updates, rescaled.
- Label floor: every victim record rebuilt as the class-average image of the public pool (all digits that no client holds).

The victim images of a seed are the first 8 entries of the seeded permutation whatever the client count, so a seed has the
same victim images at 4 and 8 clients. Only the other clients and the size of the public pool change.
Evaluation seeds are 0 to 29. Per-client seeds use `intervals.client_seed`. Primary SecAgg+ setting is `max_weight` 1000 (the upstream default), fixed before the 8-client run.
`max_weight` 16 is reported as a secondary setting.

Pass rules are applied to point estimates of the pooled recognisable rate (as C2 and C3 were in v0.14.3). Wilson intervals are pooled
over the 240 victim images. Images of one seed are not independent, so the seed-level bootstrap interval (4,000 resamples) is shown too.

## S1 and S2: results (30 seeds, 240 victim images per row)

Rate of recognisable victim images, Wilson 95% interval in brackets, mean per-image MSE after it.

| View | 4 clients | 8 clients |
|---|---|---|
| Label floor (class average) | 75.0% [69.2, 80.1], MSE 0.0414 | 74.6% [68.7, 79.7], MSE 0.0415 |
| Plaintext individual update | 95.0% [91.5, 97.1], MSE 0.0128 | 95.0% [91.5, 97.1], MSE 0.0128 |
| n-1 collusion (max_weight 1000) | 90.4% [86.0, 93.5], MSE 0.0211 | 90.8% [86.5, 93.9], MSE 0.0234 |
| Aggregate only (max_weight 1000) | 46.3% [40.1, 52.6], MSE 0.0662 | 35.0% [29.2, 41.2], MSE 0.0730 |
| n-1 collusion (max_weight 16) | 95.8% [92.5, 97.7], MSE 0.0123 | 95.8% [92.5, 97.7], MSE 0.0129 |
| Aggregate only (max_weight 16) | 55.0% [48.7, 61.2], MSE 0.0621 | 39.6% [33.6, 45.9], MSE 0.0667 |

Plaintext is the same at both sizes because the victim and the victim step are identical.

**S1 PASS (vacuous, by the one-sided rule).** The aggregate view at 8 clients is 35.0% against a floor of 74.6%, a gap of -39.6 points (seed bootstrap [-47.5, -32.1]).
The rule needs at most +5 points. Even the upper Wilson limit (41.2%) is far below the floor.
The prediction text says the aggregate "stays at the floor" but the rule is one-sided, so an attack far below the floor passes. A two-sided reading (within 5 points of the floor) would FAIL S1.
The pass is therefore about how weak this attack is, not privacy evidence. See `docs/preregistration/v0.15.md`, deviation log entry 14.

**S2 PASS.** n-1 collusion is 90.4% at 4 clients and 90.8% at 8 clients. Both Wilson lower limits (86.0% and 86.5%) are above 85%.

How to read S1 honestly. The pass does not say the aggregate is private. The aggregate attack is weaker than the label floor
at both sizes, and it gets weaker with more clients. A likely reason is that the attack has to separate 64 images from one summed gradient, but this was not tested. Mean MSE of the
aggregate view at 8 clients (0.073) is about the same as the mean image (0.072). It says this attack, with these settings, learns less than
the labels alone give. A better attack on the aggregate is not ruled out, so any statement that the aggregate sits at or below the floor is conditional on this weaker attack (see also `docs/v0.15-external-baselines.md`). The collusion result is the real finding: once n-1 clients cooperate,
the victim is as recoverable at 8 clients as at 4 (the pre-registered "stays at plaintext level" is met at 85%, the observed gap to plaintext is about 4 points).

Reproduction check. Running the same code with the legacy per-client seeds (`seed * 10 + client`) on seeds 0 to 9 at 4 clients reproduces
every saved v0.14.2 victim MSE exactly (50 view and seed pairs, maximum difference 0.0). See `scaling/legacy_seed_check/summary_legacy.json`.
The 30-seed label floor is 75.0%, not the 67.5% of v0.14.3, because seeds 10 to 29 are easier. On seeds 0 to 9 the floor is 67.5% (54 of 80 images, reproduced exactly) and on seeds 10 to 29 it is 78.8% (126 of 160). The per-seed spread of the floor is large (standard deviation 14.3 points over the 30 seeds). The client seeds have no effect here, because the local step is plain SGD without DP noise (new and legacy client seeds give identical victim MSE on seeds 0 to 9, maximum difference 0.0).
Gaps to the floor over 30 seeds, in points: plaintext +20.0 at 4 clients and +20.4 at 8, n-1 collusion (max_weight 1000) +15.4 and +16.3, aggregate only (max_weight 1000) -28.8 and -39.6. The v0.14.3 numbers (seeds 0 to 9, 4 clients) stay as published.

## Fewer shares than clients

`secagg_server.main` used to demand `num-shares == clients`. With 8 clients every client then shares its seed with all seven others.
SecAgg+ is designed for smaller neighbourhoods. The check now lives in `src/feddisclosure/training/secagg_params.py`:

- unchanged: at least 3 clients and `2 <= threshold <= shares <= clients`. With `shares == clients` the old rule is reproduced exactly (a test compares against the old rule over a grid).
- new, only when `shares < clients`: shares at least 3 and odd, and `threshold < shares`.
- `threshold == shares` is still accepted by this check when `shares == clients` (kept for backwards compatibility with the old rule). It is rejected when the upstream SecAgg+ workflow is built, as the n4-t4 dropout-matrix run recorded (`reports/results/secagg/v014_dropout_matrix/`), so use `threshold < shares`.
- more shares than clients is rejected as before.

Tests (`tests/training/test_secagg_params.py`) cover valid and invalid combinations, agreement with the upstream `SecAggPlusWorkflow` constructor on a grid,
and that the server rejects bad values before touching the grid and hands `num_shares=7, threshold=4` to the upstream workflow for 8 clients.
A security note, not enforced: for the neighbourhood construction to resist a malicious server the threshold should exceed half the neighbourhood size.

## S3: native Docker, 8 clients

Files: `compose.flower.n8.yaml` (generated by `scripts/gen_compose_clients.py 8`, same rules as the 4-client file), `configs/training/fedavg_digits_iid_8clients_1round.yaml`,
`scripts/run_scaling_n8.py`, `src/feddisclosure/evaluation/scaling_memory.py`, `.github/workflows/scaling-n8.yml`.

`run_scaling_n8.py` wraps `scripts/run_flower_docker.py` without changing it (compose startup, topology certification, the Flower CLI run, receipt checks, teardown)
and adds a `docker stats` poll with a requested 2 second interval. In practice `docker stats` is slow with 19 containers and the polls were about 4 seconds apart. The shared preparation helper accepts only three or four clients, so staging for eight is done in the new script.
It writes `scaling_n8_memory.json` with the peak in GB (10^9 bytes), the peak of one container, the container count and a pass flag.
Pass needs a finished and attested run, every one of the 18 expected container names seen (clientexec and supernode for each of 8 clients, serverexec and superlink, extra containers such as the CLI one are allowed), and a peak total below 6 GB at one poll. Docker reports usage without page cache and a spike shorter than the poll
interval can be missed, so the number is a lower bound.

### Result

PASS. One 8-client SecAgg+ round (8 shares, threshold 5) finished and was attested on a 4 CPU, 16.8 GB hosted runner. The peak of the summed container memory was 3.25 GB at one poll, against the 6 GB rule. The largest single container peaked at 0.32 GB (a ClientApp executor). 19 containers were seen, the 18 expected plus the CLI container. Evidence: `reports/results/privacy/v0150/scaling/docker_n8/scaling_n8_memory.json`, check in `prereg_check_scaling.json`.

A second hosted run with proper neighbourhoods (8 clients, 7 shares, threshold 4, run 45084177134) also finished and was attested, with a peak total of 3.16 GB and 19 containers seen. It confirms the new shares-below-clients check is accepted by the upstream workflow in a native round. Evidence: `reports/results/privacy/v0150/scaling/docker_n8/scaling_n8_memory_7_4.json`.

Polling: the first run (8 shares, threshold 5) has 11 polls and the second (7 shares, threshold 4) has 14. Spacing was 4.1 to 4.4 seconds in the first run and 4.1 to 5.8 seconds in the second, not 2. The summed total swings by up to 1.2 GB between adjacent polls in the first run (1.7 GB in the second), so the true peak is probably higher than the sampled 3.25 GB. The margin to the 6 GB rule is 2.75 GB at the sampled peak, which is large but not a bound. The rule was read as the sum over all containers, which is stricter than a per-container reading (the largest container was 0.32 GB).

Limits: 11 and 14 polls about 4 seconds apart over a short single round, so the peak is a lower bound. The model is the small Digits MLP. The Fashion-MNIST CNN was not run in Docker. Memory says nothing about privacy at 8 clients, which comes from the observation model above.

Dispatch (the workflow is on `main`, so dispatch it from `main`):

```
gh workflow run scaling-n8.yml --ref main                       # 8 shares, threshold 5 (the 4-client convention)
gh workflow run scaling-n8.yml --ref main -f num_shares=7 -f threshold=4   # proper neighbourhoods, exercises the new check natively
```

After a run, put `scaling_n8_memory.json` under `reports/results/privacy/v0150/scaling/docker_n8/` and rerun `python scripts/write_prereg_checks_wp9_wp10.py`.
If the peak is 6 GB or more, S3 is FAIL and the 8-client Docker run is dropped, as the pre-registration says.

## Limits

- Observation-model study with real quantiser arithmetic. It is not an attack on captured traffic from an 8-client Docker run.
- One local step, 8 records per client, known labels, known initial model and learning rate.
- 8x8 digits from one dataset. The second dataset is in `docs/v0.15-fashion-mnist.md`.
- Seeds are independent data draws, but images of one seed share an initial model, so Wilson intervals pooled over images are somewhat optimistic. The seed bootstrap intervals are about as wide.
