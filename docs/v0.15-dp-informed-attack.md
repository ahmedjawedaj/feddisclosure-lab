# v0.15 WP5: informed attacker against record-level DP-SGD, one full-batch step

This is a replication, in the federated update setting, of the reconstruction-robustness analysis of Hayes, Mahloujifar and Balle (arXiv 2302.07225, "Bounding Training Data Reconstruction in DP-SGD"). It is not a new bound and it is not a new attack. The aim is to put three numbers next to each other for the same setup: what a concrete informed attacker achieves, the exact best an attacker could achieve on the candidate sets that were actually used, and the closed form upper bound.

Pre-registered predictions D1 to D5 are in `docs/preregistration/v0.15.md`. Results are in `reports/results/privacy/v0150/prereg_check_dp.json`.

## Setup

- Data: the 1,797 public 8x8 digits (`sklearn.load_digits`, pixels divided by 16), model `DigitMLP` (2,410 parameters), a fresh random initialisation per trial.
- Client: 8 records, one full-batch local step with the real Opacus 1.6.0 `PrivacyEngine` (no Poisson sampling, the batch is the whole client dataset), clipping norm C = 1.0, learning rate 0.1, noise multiplier sigma in {0.2, 0.5, 1, 2, 4}. Opacus adds N(0, (sigma C)^2) noise to the sum of the clipped per-sample gradients.
- Attacker: knows the model initialisation, the learning rate, C, sigma, the other 7 records and a list of u candidates for the eighth record. The list contains the true record. It subtracts the clipped gradients of the 7 known records from the observed update and gets a residual R.
- Candidates: drawn from the public pool, which is every record that is not one of the client's 8 records. The only candidate that is also a client record is the true victim, inserted once. Same-label candidates share the victim's label (u 10 and 100). Any-label candidates are drawn from the whole pool (u 10, 100 and 1000). Candidate sets are nested for a given trial and the true record sits at a random position.
- Trials: 1,000 per cell on evaluation seeds 0 to 999. One real Opacus step per (seed, sigma) is scored against every candidate set. No tuning seed (9000 and up) was used and nothing was tuned.
- Attacks on the real Opacus output. Inner product (Hayes Algorithm 2): score each candidate by the inner product of R with its clipped gradient and take the argmax. This is the primary attack. Nearest candidate: the candidate whose clipped gradient is closest to R in Euclidean distance, which is the Bayes optimal rule for equal priors and Gaussian noise.
- Exact optimal: the success probability of the nearest-candidate rule on the actual candidate sets, by Monte Carlo over the noise only (candidate gradients are fixed given the initialisation). 40 noise draws per trial, so 40,000 draws per cell.
- Orthogonal-candidate reference: the exact success of the optimal rule if the u clipped gradients were mutually orthogonal with norm C, which is P(Z0 + 1/sigma > max of u-1 standard normals). It is an extra column that separates the looseness of the bound from the effect of the real candidate geometry.
- Intervals: Clopper-Pearson 95 percent from `feddisclosure.evaluation.intervals`.

Scope note. An FL server does not see intermediate gradients. A client that takes several local steps is a stronger threat model for the defender, because the attacker would have to cope with unseen iterates while the noise adds up over steps. That case is out of scope here.

## The bound and its adjacency

Adjacency is add or remove one record, which is the adjacency Opacus accounts for. If the victim is candidate i, the residual is N(c_i, (sigma C)^2 I) where c_i is the clipped gradient of candidate i and the norm of c_i is at most C. The reference world has the victim record removed, so the residual is N(0, (sigma C)^2 I). The reference is an analysis device only and it is never run.

1. For any answer event E_i of the attacker, deciding between N(c_i, .) and N(0, .) is a Gaussian shift problem with standardised shift at most mu = 1/sigma. By the Neyman-Pearson lemma, P_i(E_i) is at most h(Q(E_i)) with h(q) = Phi(Phi^-1(q) + 1/sigma), where Q is the reference distribution.
2. The events E_1 to E_u partition the output space, so the Q(E_i) sum to 1 and average to 1/u.
3. The function h is concave, because its derivative exp(-mu Phi^-1(q) - mu^2 / 2) falls as q grows. By Jensen, the average of h(Q(E_i)) is at most h(1/u).

So for u equally likely candidates

    success <= Phi( Phi^-1(1/u) + 1/sigma ).

Sensitivity is C and not 2C. Two candidates can be 2C apart in the worst case (antipodal clipped gradients), but the argument compares each candidate with the absent record and never candidate with candidate. The bound is tight in general: for u = 2 with antipodal clipped gradients the optimal rule achieves exactly Phi(1/sigma), and a unit test checks that. The probe that preceded this work used the same expression, here it is derived and the derivation is the one above.

The bound does not use an accountant, a delta or composition. It describes one invocation of one Gaussian mechanism with no subsampling amplification. It says nothing about several steps.

## Checks that the real Opacus step is what the analysis assumes

`reports/results/privacy/v0150/dp_informed/crosscheck.json` and the unit tests compare the real Opacus output with a hand computed clipped sum (independent per-sample gradients through `torch.func`, same clipping rule). At sigma 0 the maximum absolute difference is at most 6e-7 (5.78e-07 in `crosscheck.json`, float32 rounding). For sigma 0.2, 1 and 4 with 300 trials each over 2,410 coordinates, the standardised residual (Opacus output minus clipped sum, divided by sigma C) has mean within 0.001 of 0, variance within 0.003 of 1 and a Kolmogorov-Smirnov test against the standard normal on all 723,000 values gives p of 0.46, 0.89 and 0.88. The hand computation is used only for this cross check and for the attacker's known-record subtraction. All reported attack numbers come from the real Opacus output.

## Results

Each cell shows the rate with its Clopper-Pearson 95 percent interval. For the exact optimal column the interval covers the Monte Carlo noise only. It does not include the choice of which 1,000 candidate sets were drawn. The per-trial standard errors are in `summary.json`.

The last column is a paired z score computed from the saved per-trial records in `trials_sigma*.jsonl`. For each trial the exact optimum gives a success probability p_i on that trial's candidate set, and z is (attack successes minus the sum of p_i) divided by the square root of the sum of p_i (1 - p_i). It compares the attack with the optimum on the same trials, which is tighter than comparing the two intervals. Across the 25 cells z runs from -2.38 to +0.77 with mean -0.52, and three cells are below -2 (any-label u 100 at sigma 0.2 and 0.5, same-label u 10 at sigma 4). The attack and the optimum are the same rule on the same candidate sets, so z should centre on 0. About 0.6 of 25 cells would fall below -2 by chance. The three observed cells and the negative mean are a mild shortfall that this report does not explain. The cells share trials and candidate sets, so they are not independent.

How the wording was read. The pre-registration says "u 10" without saying which candidate mode, so D2 and D3 require both modes to pass, and D4 requires all five candidate sets to pass. D5's "up to Monte Carlo error" was read as stated in the last row. This two-mode rule is recorded in the script (`src/feddisclosure/attacks/dp_informed.py`, `prereg_check`) and in the results file, which were committed together in c73dcb9. It was not separately timestamped before the run, so it cannot be shown to predate the results. The thresholds themselves were not touched. The `[post-probe]` thresholds of D2 and D3 came from a feasibility probe. The probe's seeds are not recorded anywhere in the repository, so it cannot be verified that they were all 9000 or above. The evaluation seeds of this report are 0 to 999. Under the stricter reading D3 fails. Under a reading that used only the any-label mode it would pass, and under a same-label-only reading (the mode of the earlier probe) it would fail. The same-label shortfall of 0.055 is 0.005 above the limit. The exact optimum for those candidate sets is 0.118, which is 0.033 below the bound, and the attack is 0.022 below that optimum. With 1,000 trials the standard error of a rate near 0.118 is about 0.010, so the 0.022 shortfall is about 2.2 sigma (paired z -2.17 in the table above). It is on the large side for sampling noise alone. The result stays FAIL as registered.

The point estimates of the attack are sometimes slightly above the exact optimum (for example any-label u 10 at sigma 1 with 0.336 against 0.325). That is binomial noise. The attack and the exact optimum are the same rule (nearest candidate, which equals inner product here) applied to the same candidate sets, so they have the same expected success. The paired z column above is the test of that.

## Where the gap to the bound comes from

Same-label gradient similarity explains most of the gap for same-label candidates, and little of it for any-label candidates at u 10.

- Same-label candidates have clipped gradients that point the same way as the victim's. Mean cosine to the victim is 0.79, against 0.01 for any-label candidates. Their exact optimum is far below the orthogonal-candidate reference at low and medium noise (same-label u 10: 0.318 below at sigma 0.2 and 0.379 below at sigma 0.5).
- Any-label u 10 is close to the orthogonal reference at every sigma (within 0.061) and so the remaining distance to the bound is the looseness of the bound itself, at most 0.09 at sigma 0.5 and at most 0.018 for sigma 2 and above.
- At sigma 4 every gap is small. The same-label u 10 gap of 0.055 splits into 0.007 for the bound, 0.026 for candidate similarity and 0.022 for the shortfall of the attack against its own exact optimum, which is about 2.2 sigma of sampling noise.
- For any-label u 100 and 1000 at low noise a share of the gap comes from candidates of the victim's own class that lie close to the victim by chance (the nearest other candidate is 0.44 C away at u 100 and 0.31 C at u 1000), and from the bound being loose for large u.
- Every per-sample gradient exceeds the clip at the random initialisation, so all clipped gradients have norm C. The inner-product and the nearest-candidate rules then pick the same candidate in every trial, and the two attack columns are identical. They would differ if some candidates had gradients shorter than C.

## What this does and does not show

- It shows that, for one full-batch step with record-level DP-SGD and an attacker who knows the other records and a candidate list, measured identification success never exceeds the closed form bound, tracks the exact optimum for the real candidate sets, and falls as sigma grows.
- It does not show a DP guarantee for the federated system. The bound covers record-level privacy of one Gaussian mechanism invocation, with add or remove adjacency. It is not client-level privacy. The attacker here has no access to anything except one client's one-step update, so it says nothing about what a server learns across rounds, from other clients or from secure aggregation.
- It uses no accountant, so it makes no epsilon or delta claim. It does not add noise composition over several steps or rounds.
- It is a replication of Hayes, Mahloujifar and Balle in this setting and the attack is their Algorithm 2 (prior-aware attack). The paper states its bound through a blow-up function that it estimates by Monte Carlo. For one Gaussian step that bound reduces to the closed form used here. The derivation above is written out for the setup that was run.
- Identification success among u candidates and reconstruction error are different metrics. A low identification rate does not say that an inversion attack without candidates fails, and a recognisable reconstruction is not the same as picking the right record from a list. The reconstruction results are in the v0.14 documents. The two should not be added up or compared as if they were one scale.
- Several local steps are a stronger threat model and are out of scope. An FL server sees only the final update, but a client that takes several steps gives the attacker a more informative object than one step, and the analysis here does not cover it.
- The data are small public digits and a 2,410-parameter model. Gradient geometry of other models and data will give other gaps between the attack and the bound.
