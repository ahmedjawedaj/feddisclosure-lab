# v0.16.9: noise delivered by the differential privacy aggregators of TensorFlow Federated 0.87.0, measured against their configuration

Status: measured on `tensorflow-federated` 0.87.0, integrated in release 0.16.9 (not yet peer reviewed).
Pre-registration: `docs/preregistration/v0.16-tff-dp.md` (commit `7a22b12`, committed alone before the script was written and before any call with noise in this phase).
Evidence: `reports/results/privacy/v0160/tff_dp/tff-0.87.0/tff_dp_audit.json`.
Script: `scripts/audit_tff_dp.py`. Tests: `tests/privacy/test_audit_tff_dp.py`.
Follows: the Flower measurements `docs/v0.16-p1-flower-dp-audit.md`, `docs/v0.16-adaptive-clipping-audit.md` and `docs/v0.16-adaptive-guarantee-audit.md`, whose checks this release mirrors for a framework that is not Flower.

## Summary

The script runs the real `DifferentiallyPrivateFactory.gaussian_fixed` and `gaussian_adaptive`, a `MeanFactory` around the fixed one, and `tff.learning.dp_aggregator`, through `process.next` in TFF's own C++ runtime. It asks whether the noise that comes out matches what the configuration says (noise multiplier times clip, divided by the configured `clients_per_round`), and whether adaptive clipping counts, moves and orders its steps as the configuration and the paper describe. No epsilon or delta is computed. The aggregators measured report a per-round `DpEvent` and no epsilon (no name containing epsilon in the six files scanned).

15 of 16 rows and 126 of 127 scripted checks held. The one failed check is a pre-registered number that was an arithmetic slip, described below. No statistical check failed, so no replication ran, and no delivered value differed from its configured value outside the stated tolerance. Clauses that no script check covers are listed in a section below and in the pre-registration.

1. Fixed clipping. The noise standard deviation per coordinate equals `noise_multiplier * clip / clients_per_round` within 0.21% (ratios 1.0021, 1.0003 and 0.9983 for three configurations, tolerance 0.5%). The noise is normal by skewness and kurtosis. The reported event is a Gaussian event with the configured multiplier.
2. Sensitivity. One client's effect on the output is exactly `clip / clients_per_round` for an update above the clip (add or remove), twice that for a replace-one pair, and the clip is global over a model of two tensors (0.1 where a per-tensor clip would give 0.1414). Differences from the prediction were below 3e-8 relative.
3. Number of clients that report. With 5 or 20 clients against `clients_per_round` 10, the noise is still `noise_multiplier * clip / clients_per_round` (ratios 0.9997 and 1.0012) and the one-client sensitivity is still `clip / clients_per_round`, so the delivered multiplier is 0.9997 and 1.0012. The aggregate is scaled by m over n (0.25 and 1.0 for updates of norm 0.5). Dividing by the reporting count would have given ratios of 0.5 and 2.0, and the check saw 0.4998 and 2.0024.
4. Weights. With `MeanFactory` around the fixed factory the noise equals `noise_multiplier * clip / (clients_per_round * W)` for the sum of weights W (ratios 0.9993 and 0.9996 for equal and unequal weights). The clip acts on the weighted update, so a client of weight 5 and update norm 0.5 is clipped to norm 1 before the division. This is a composition that the types allow. The files read do not present it as a DP recipe.
5. What the state reports. Each round holds one Gaussian event (fixed) or a composed event of two Gaussian events (adaptive), for that round only. There is no sampling event, no adjacency field and no epsilon. A scan of six source files found no name containing "epsilon" outside docstrings and comments.
6. Adaptive clipping, bit and order. The next clip equals `clip * exp(-0.2 * (u - 0.5))` for the share u of clients below the clip, within 1e-5, so the clip falls when more clients are below it. This is the direction of the paper. A norm equal to the clip counts as below it. The noise and the clipped sum use the clip that the clients used, and the clip is updated afterwards. The delivered noise matches the old clip within 0.12% and would be 10% higher than the value for the new clip (mean ratio 1.1003 and 1.1005).
7. Adaptive clipping, budget split. The value multiplier is `(z^-2 - (2 sigma_b)^-2)^-0.5` with `sigma_b` 0.05 times `clients_per_round` by default. The reported event has multipliers equal to that value and to `2 sigma_b`, and they compose to the configured z within 1e-7 (relative). The delivered count noise has standard deviation 5.068 against 5.0 (n 100, R 2,000) and 0.6045 against 0.6 (n 12, R 8,000). The composed delivered multiplier, from the delivered sum noise and delivered count noise, is 0.9989 for n 100 and 1.0049 for n 12.
8. Cohort size and the counted fraction. The counted fraction is computed against the configured number of clients, so with 50 of 100 clients all below the clip it is 0.75 and not 1, and the clip falls by `exp(-0.05)` where a full cohort gives `exp(-0.1)`. With 200 of 100 the fraction 1.5 is clamped to 1. The delivered sum noise at 50 of 100 clients is still `z_value * clip / 100` (ratio 0.9984, against 0.4992 for a division by the reporting count).
9. `dp_aggregator`. Its noise matches the factory (ratio 1.0001) and its noise-free clip path equals the factory's. The default adaptive zeroing replaces a client whose largest coordinate is above 10 by zeros before clipping, and the zeroed client then counts as below the clip. With 50 clients above the clip and 50 zeroed, the next clip is 0.1 (fraction 0.5) and the zeroed count is 50. With `zeroing=False` all 100 are above the clip and the next clip is 0.11051710.
10. Randomness. Two calls from one state give uncorrelated noise (correlation 0.0034, lag-1 autocorrelation -0.0030, threshold 0.0126). Two fresh processes that each call `tf.random.set_seed(12345)` and `numpy.random.seed(12345)` before building the process give different outputs, so the Python global seed does not control the noise. A rerun of the script reproduces the verdicts and not the noise values.
11. The whole script took 408.5 seconds on one machine (2 cores).

These are measurements of one TFF version in stated configurations. They say nothing about the guarantee of a trained run, other TFF aggregators or other versions, and no adjacency relation is stated by the TFF documentation read.

## Setup

`tensorflow-federated` 0.87.0 (the newest release on PyPI, uploaded 2024-09-17, requires Python below 3.12) in a Python 3.11.18 virtual environment with `tensorflow` 2.14.1, `tensorflow-privacy` 0.9.0, `dp-accounting` 0.4.3, `numpy` 1.25.2, `jax` 0.4.14, `jaxlib` 0.4.14 and `scipy` 1.9.3. 0.87.0 pins `jaxlib` 0.4.14, which is not on PyPI, so the install used `uv pip install --find-links https://storage.googleapis.com/jax-releases/jax_releases.html tensorflow-federated==0.87.0`. The CI environment of this repository has no TFF, so the tests that call it skip there. TFF runs the aggregation in a C++ worker process that it starts, and the script ends it when it finishes.

Noise rows send zero vectors of 100,000 float32 coordinates, so the output is the noise. The statistic is the root mean square over all coordinates of all calls, with relative standard error 1 over the square root of 2N. Noise-free rows use noise multiplier 0.0, which the constructors accept, and compare outputs in float64 with tolerance 1e-5 relative. The new clip is read from the measurement `dp_query_metrics.clip` and the counted fraction is recovered as 0.5 minus ln(C1 / C0) over the learning rate 0.2. Adjacency is not stated by the TFF documentation read. The reading used is add or remove one client, with sensitivity C for the sum and 1/2 for the count (the TFP source centres each bit at plus or minus 1/2). Replace-one is reported where a row can show it.

## Power of each null result

For an RMS check the decision threshold is a fixed tolerance, so a true deviation d is found with 80% power when d is at least the tolerance plus 0.8416 standard errors. The script recomputed these numbers and they matched the pre-registered ones.

| Null result | Standard error | Decision threshold | Smallest deviation found with 80% power | Informative by the pre-registered rule |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| F1, F3, F5, A6, A8 noise RMS (300,000 values) | 0.129% | 0.5% | 0.609% | yes (at most 1%) |
| A4 noise RMS (1,000,000 values) | 0.0707% | 0.5% | 0.560% | yes |
| A5a count standard deviation (R 2,000) | 1.58% | 6% | 7.3% | yes (at most 10%) |
| A5c count standard deviation (R 8,000) | 0.79% | 4% | 4.7% | yes |
| A7, n 100 | 0.072% | 1% | 1.06% | yes (at most 3%) |
| A7, n 12 | 0.55% | 2.5% | 2.96% | yes, with little room |
| F6 correlations (100,000 coordinates) | 0.00316 | 0.01265 | 0.0153 | yes |
| Exact checks | float32 rounding below 1e-6 | 1e-5 relative | any difference above 1e-5 on the inputs tried | yes for those inputs |

A deviation of the size that the readings in this work turn on is far above these limits. A division by the reporting count changes the noise by a factor 2, a clip used after its update changes it by 10%, and a counted fraction against the arrived count moves it by 0.25. None of these was seen. The positive controls (F3 check 2, A4 check 2, A6 noisy check, F6 control, the A3 boundary pair) can each register a deviation of that size, and each did so for the alternative reading and not for TFF's.

Each statistical check has a false failure probability of about 1e-4 when the delivered noise equals the prediction. The script has 37 such checks, so about 0.4% chance of one false failure in a run. This estimate was made by hand.

What a pass covers. Zero update vectors for the noise rows, a small set of fixed update norms for the clip rows, the stated n and m, one TFF version and platform, float32 vectors with one tensor (and one two-tensor case in F2). It does not cover other norms, a trained model, float64 inputs, ragged inputs, other aggregators or the sampling of clients.

## Clauses that no script check covers

These are read or judged by hand. They are listed in the pre-registration and named in every summary of this release.

1. The statements about the source code, the probe output and the install route (pre-registration "What was already known").
2. The five quotes under "Claimed behaviour" in the pre-registration. The tutorial page text came from one page fetch and was not checked word for word.
3. That 0.87.0 is the newest release on PyPI and was uploaded on 2024-09-17.
4. The paper reading (bit direction, order of noise and clip update, budget split), taken from one earlier read of the paper.
5. That the TFF documentation read states no adjacency relation. This is an absence claim over the pages read.
6. That `MeanFactory` around the DP factory is allowed by the constructor types and is not shown as a DP example in the files read.
7. That the scan for "epsilon" names covers six files only and that nothing else in TFF reports an epsilon. The scan of the whole package is reported in the evidence and not judged. The file `python/analytics/differential_privacy.py` holds `analytic_gauss_stddev(epsilon, delta, norm_bound)`, which takes epsilon and delta and returns a noise standard deviation. It was read after the run and is not used by the aggregators.
8. The false failure probability estimate and the "What a pass covers" paragraph.
9. That the C++ worker process runs the same TensorFlow kernels as the Python side.
10. The wording of the README, this document and the comparison below.

## What differs from the Flower measurements

By hand reading, for the configurations tried, and not a statement about intent.

| Question | Flower (earlier releases of this repository) | TFF 0.87.0 |
|---|---|---|
| Fewer results than the configured count, fixed clipping | the noise multiplier against the clients' sensitivity falls (row C4 of v0.16.0) | noise and sensitivity both use the configured count, so the multiplier stays at z. The mean is scaled by m over n. |
| Unequal weights | the multiplier falls for a client with a large share (row C3) | the factory is unweighted. Under `MeanFactory` the weighted update is clipped, so every client has the same sensitivity bound |
| Bit that feeds the clip update | set for clipped updates, so the clip moves away from the target quantile (v0.16.3) | set for updates below the clip, which is the paper's direction |
| Order of the clip update and noise | the noise uses the clip after the update (v0.16.3) | the noise uses the clip the clients used, and the update comes after |
| Released count | plain sum of 0 or 1 bits (v0.16.6) | sum of bits centred at plus or minus 1/2, with the budget split written for sensitivity 1/2 |
| Composed delivered multiplier | 0.8952 of configured in the default cell (v0.16.6) | 0.9989 and 1.0049 of configured in the two cells tried, inside the stated tolerance |
| Accounting | an optional accountant in 1.44.0 (v0.16.0) | a per-round `DpEvent` and no epsilon in the aggregators measured |

## Limits

1. One TFF version (0.87.0). Earlier releases were not run. TFF's repository activity after this release was not checked.
2. The aggregation ran in TFF's C++ runtime on CPU. A different runtime or an accelerator was not tried.
3. The noise rows use zero updates, and the clip rows use two fixed norms. A trained model with data dependent norms was not run, and no row shows what the clip trajectory does under learning.
4. The tree aggregation constructors, distributed DP, secure aggregation records and `record_aggregation_factory` other than the sum were not measured.
5. No adjacency relation is documented, no epsilon was computed and nothing here certifies a privacy guarantee. A delivered multiplier equal to the configured one is a statement about the noise, and the sampling of clients, the composition over rounds and the adjacency convention stay with the user.
6. The count noise was measured through the clip update (a recovery of the noisy fraction from the new clip), which depends on the geometric update formula. The clamp of the fraction was seen in A5b and A6.
7. The statistics use normal approximations. The noise could not be seeded, so the saved evidence is one run.
8. The release gate runs in CI on pull requests and pushes to main are not gated.

## Reproduce

```bash
uv venv --python 3.11 tff_venv
uv pip install --python tff_venv/bin/python --find-links https://storage.googleapis.com/jax-releases/jax_releases.html tensorflow-federated==0.87.0
tff_venv/bin/python scripts/audit_tff_dp.py               # about 7 minutes, writes the evidence file
tff_venv/bin/python scripts/audit_tff_dp.py --quick --out out/   # about 1 minute, not evidence
tff_venv/bin/python -m pytest tests/privacy/test_audit_tff_dp.py
```
