# v0.16.5: the selection step of NVIDIA FLARE's SVTPrivacy, measured with stated power

Status: measured on `nvflare` 2.9.1, integrated in release 0.16.5 (not yet peer reviewed).
Pre-registration: `docs/preregistration/v0.16-nvflare-selection.md` (commit `cdd2ce031f7da61d642f1afe5b20f950a90dab08`, committed alone before the script existed and before any real call of the filter in this phase).
Evidence: `reports/results/privacy/v0160/nvflare_selection/nvflare_selection.json`.
Script: `scripts/audit_nvflare_selection.py`. Tests: `tests/privacy/test_audit_nvflare_selection.py`.

## Summary

Row N6b of the v0.16.4 audit tested the selection phase with a claim of 20 and 200,000 runs per input. At that run count no outcome can give a 95% lower bound above about 10.9, so the check could not fail at its claim, and its recorded value (0.871) said little about a violation. This release replaces it with three configurations where the claim is small enough that a violation could be seen, and states for every null result the smallest excess that the check would have found with 80% power.

Each configuration calls the real `SVTPrivacy.process_dxo` 1,000,000 times (500,000 per input, in 5 blocks of 100,000 with their own seeds). The event is that the set of nonzero output entries equals the first n entries. The pair of inputs is the most adverse one under the reading that each coordinate has sensitivity gamma: the first n entries at gamma and the rest at 0, against the first n entries at 0 and the rest at gamma.

All 7 rows and all 44 scripted checks held. One unscripted prediction (the log ratio as a fraction of the claim) held only partly, see Predictions against results. No lower bound exceeded its claim, so no replication was run.

1. The old check had zero power at its claim. With 1.25% per tail the ceiling at 200,000 runs is 10.73 (10.90 with the 2.5% tails of v0.16.4), below the claim of 20 (row S0).
2. Lower bounds on the epsilon of the event were 0.418, 0.357 and 0.015 against claims of 1, 1 and 0.1 (rows S1 to S3). S2 uses `replace` True, which is the class default.
3. The smallest event epsilon that the check would have found with 80% power is 1.02, 1.03 and 0.126 against those claims, an excess of 2.1%, 2.6% and 26% of the claim. A pass is therefore informative to about a quarter of the claim or better in all three rows.
4. A numeric computation that uses only the formulas of the acceptance loop (no `nvflare` code) gives probabilities of the event that the measured counts match within 2.1 binomial standard errors in all six worlds. It gives log ratios of 0.451, 0.400 and 0.441 times the claim. The measured point estimates are 0.441, 0.386 and 0.425 times the claim.
5. The two filter files are byte identical in the wheels of 2.8.3, 2.9.0 and 2.9.1, and one seeded call of each filter returned the same digest from each version.
6. The run took 530.1 seconds.

These are measurements of one event under one pair of inputs in small configurations. They are lower bounds on the epsilon of the filter and do not certify the claim for other events, pairs or sizes.

## Setup

`nvflare` 2.9.1 in a separate virtual environment (Python 3.13.18, numpy 2.5.5, Linux, one core). The harness imports the filter and the DXO, Shareable and FLContext classes. No NVFlare server, client, job or training loop was started.

Inputs are float64 vectors named `w` with gamma 1e-5. S1 and S2 use d 6 and `fraction` 0.3, so n is 2, with `epsilon` 1. S3 uses d 6 and `fraction` 0.5, so n is 3, with `epsilon` 0.1. Other parameters are the class defaults. The claim is the ledger `epsilon_threshold` plus `epsilon_query` of the first call, which equals `epsilon`. The ledger `n_upload` was 2, 2 and 3 as intended.

Block seeds are 20261126 + 10000 r + 100 w + b, with r the row number, w the world and b the block. A fresh filter instance is made every 10,000 calls. The interval is Clopper-Pearson with 1.25% in each tail, using the pure Python helper of `scripts/audit_nvflare_dp.py`. The lower bound is the largest of four log ratios (both directions, the event and its complement), which keeps the family error at 5%. This differs from v0.16.4, which used 2.5% per tail, and the change was pre-registered.

The numeric computation integrates over the threshold noise on 3,000 quantile points and sums over the pass at which the acceptance loop stops, with the accepted entries selected uniformly. It was written before the pre-registration and is reproduced in the script. It shares my reading of the loop, so it checks the filter against that reading and not against an independent source.

## Power of each null result

The statements use N 500,000 per world, the normal approximation of the lower bound and the denominator probability from the numeric computation. They are for the event and pair above.

| Null result | Claim | Smallest event epsilon found with 80% power | Excess over the claim | Reading |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| S0, the old N6b | 20 | none, the ceiling is 10.73 | not detectable at any effect | Zero power. |
| S1, lower bound at most claim | 1 | 1.021 | 0.021 (2.1%) | Informative. |
| S2, lower bound at most claim | 1 | 1.026 | 0.026 (2.6%) | Informative. |
| S3, lower bound at most claim | 0.1 | 0.126 | 0.026 (26%) | Informative to about a quarter of the claim. |
| Positive control at 0.1 times the claim | 0.1, 0.1 and 0.01 | power 1.0, 1.0 and 0.952 at the numeric truth | not applicable | The check can register an excess. |
| Block agreement | not applicable | a shift of one block rate by 3 binomial standard errors is rejected with power about 0.3 | not applicable | Weak. |
| V, no byte difference | not applicable | any one byte difference in the six files | not applicable | A census of six files, so power 1 for those files only. |
| V, no difference in one seeded call | not applicable | a difference that changes the output on that one input | not applicable | Branches the input does not reach are not tested, and equal bytes already cover them. |

At the claim itself the probability of reporting an excess is below 0.001 in all three rows when the true value is the numeric one. A pass does not cover the release values, other pairs, larger d or n, values between 0 and gamma, or other events. The epsilon of the filter is the largest over all events and pairs, so the quantity checked is a lower bound on it.

## Version identity

`pip download --no-deps --only-binary=:all:` fetched `nvflare` 2.8.3 (3,264,209 bytes), 2.9.0 (3,619,563 bytes) and 2.9.1 (4,017,289 bytes). Each wheel was unpacked into a temporary directory that was deleted at the end. The sha256 of `svt_privacy.py` is `c543bbea2ce3bfe2e35a1d85a26acac8dffc0e95024e777010cdbf0cd0f92858` and of `percentile_privacy.py` is `29bcf457e2c20ff8e5fe92411673b128b0c85a94efd9a0513a102095bdbb58c0` in all three, the same values that v0.16.4 recorded for 2.9.1. The zip members and the unpacked files gave equal hashes.

One seeded call per filter ran in a child process with `python -I`, with the unpacked wheel first on the path and a check that `nvflare` was imported from there. The SVT call (`fraction` 0.25, `epsilon` 1, 64 values, seed 20261126) gave digest `164c02b3d9e5...` and the percentile call (percentile 10, gamma 0.01, 10,000 values) gave `2cda81ca3b5e...` in all three versions. The other dependencies came from the 2.9.1 environment, including numpy 2.5.5, so this says that the two filter files behave identically in that environment and does not test them with each release's own dependency pins. No difference was found.

## Limits

1. One event, one pair and small sizes (d 6, n 2 or 3). The numeric scan that chose these configurations covered n from 1 to 4 and d up to 8 with `replace` False. In that scan the log ratio of the exact selected set stayed between 0.25 and 0.48 times the claim. The rows sit near the top of the scan, and they are not a random sample.
2. The neighbouring definition is a reading. Neither the documentation nor the code states one.
3. The Monte Carlo ran only on 2.9.1. Version identity is by file hash and one seeded call.
4. The numeric computation shares my reading of the acceptance loop.
5. The lower bound is a statement about the event and pair. The power numbers use a normal approximation and the numeric denominator probability.
6. Quick test runs and a test call of the version step were made before the full run. They are in the deviation log.
7. The gate pins are not enforced in CI, because the workflows do not run the gate, and setting GATE_REPIN=1 makes the integrity check pass while it prints the diff against the base. A reviewer has to read that diff.
8. The history cannot prove that no run happened before the commit order shows. The pre-registration commit was made before the script was written.
