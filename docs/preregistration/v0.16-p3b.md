# v0.16 P3b pre-registration: a plaintext baseline above the floor before aggregate and collusion are read

Status: written before the P3b script exists and before any P3b attack call on a changed setup. Date: 2026-11-26. The commit that adds this file is the timestamp.
Subject: `breaching` 0.1.2 on real CIFAR-10 with the small CNN of P3, in the setup of P3 with the changes listed below. Tracker item T6b.
Script: `scripts/p3b_breaching_cifar10.py` (written after this file). Runbook: `docs/v0.16-p3b-colab-runbook.md`. Power table script: `scripts/p3b_power_table.py`, SHA-256 `420ee1ce93624c5cd6f691d2aee47b94ac900dca2f249aec4e1ba7895c9b7152` (it exists in the working tree and is committed after this file, with this exact content).

## Question

In P3 (v0.16.1) `breaching` did not beat the label-only floor in PSNR on the plaintext view with 8 records per client (gain -0.12 dB, interval -0.72 to +0.42). The aggregate and collusion rows of P3 therefore had nothing to be read against, and the aggregate pass was vacuous. P3b asks two questions in a fixed order.

1. Is there a batch size per client, among 4 and 2 records with one local step, at which the plaintext view clears the label-only floor by a stated margin, in PSNR and in SSIM?
2. Only for an arm and a metric where the answer to question 1 is yes: does n-1 collusion keep the attack, what does SecAgg+ quantisation cost it, and does the aggregate view fall back to the floor?

It does not claim anything about privacy. It measures one library release, one small CNN, one local step and the settings below. The aggregate rows speak only about 8 and 16 mixed images. P3 had 32 and is not re-read here.

## What was already known before this file was written

Stated so that predictions are not presented as blind.

1. The P3 results were read in full: `docs/v0.16-p3-breaching-results.md` and the saved files in `reports/results/privacy/v0160/p3_breaching/`. Numbers used here: control (batch of 1) gain +5.50 dB and SSIM gain +0.444. Plaintext at 8 records: PSNR gain -0.12 dB, SSIM gain +0.116. Aggregate: -1.47 dB and SSIM -0.007. Collusion: -0.01 dB and SSIM +0.124. Library default 24,000 iterations at 8 records: +0.46 dB, SSIM +0.182. Observed seed level standard deviation of the PSNR gain 1.32 dB (plaintext, 8 images per seed), 1.86 dB (control, 4 images per seed). Per image standard deviation of the PSNR gain 3.2 dB and of the SSIM gain 0.215. Paired standard deviation over seeds of plaintext minus collusion 0.60 dB and 0.037 SSIM, of plaintext minus aggregate 1.17 dB and 0.080 SSIM.
2. The P3 tuning table was read. Mean tuning PSNR of `breaching` for the plaintext view at total variation 0.2, 0.05, 0.01, 0.001 was 12.77, 12.73, 11.16, 10.46, so the curve is flat between 0.2 and 0.05. For the aggregate view it was 11.57, 10.43, 9.63, 8.98 and still rising at the edge. A larger total variation is therefore a weak lever for the plaintext view and a possible one for the aggregate view.
3. Because P3 and these tuning numbers were read, the SSIM margins below were chosen after seeing P3 SSIM numbers. This is a disclosed choice and not a blind one. The PSNR margin of +1.0 dB is the P3 value.
4. No attack was run for P3b on any setup with 2 or 4 records per client or with a total variation above 0.2, on real or synthetic data, before this file. A CPU smoke test on synthetic stand-in data follows this commit. It tests that the code runs and informs no parameter.
5. The evaluation seeds (100 to 119) and tuning seeds (9100 to 9102) were not used by P3 (0 to 19 and 9000 to 9002) or by any other run of this repository. The P3 designer knew the floor values and attack results of P3 seeds 0 to 19, and fresh seeds keep that knowledge out of the evaluation images.
6. Third party status of `breaching` is as recorded in `docs/preregistration/v0.16-p3.md` item 5. Release 0.1.2, wheel SHA-256 a838348beb9981bc680ff6d922d38e854a7e7fabe7afd1409befb4da5dbc06e9. It was not re-read for P3b.
7. The power numbers below come from `scripts/p3b_power_table.py`, a simulation that reads no study data. Its assumed standard deviations come from item 1, scaled up for fewer images per seed and rounded up.

## Design choices and what was dropped

The decision is to give the plaintext view a fair chance first. A fixed order of reading is built into the rules (the gate rows), so a repeat of the vacuous pass is ruled out in advance.

| Lever | Decision | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Smaller batch per client | Used. Two arms, 4 and 2 records per client, N = 4 clients | The control (1 record) was +5.50 dB and 8 records was -0.12 dB, so batch size is the one lever with a known large effect. The unknowns are 3,072 pixels times the batch against 25,578 parameters. Counting equations is only a heuristic and proves nothing. The aggregate of N clients is the exact gradient of the mean loss over N times B images (a P3 unit test shows this for B = 8), so 8 and 16 mixed images remain a harder problem than the plaintext view |
| Wider total variation grid | Used. Grid 0.05, 0.2, 0.8, 3.2, factor 4 apart, 4,000 iterations, tuned at 4 records | P3 chose the edge (0.2). The grid now reaches 16 times higher. 0.05 stays because small batches may prefer less smoothing. 0.01 and 0.001 are dropped because they lost in P3 |
| SSIM as a second metric | Used. Own margin and own gate per arm | PSNR rewards smooth images and the class average is smooth. P3 SSIM gains disagreed in sign with PSNR for two rows |
| More local steps | Dropped | The shipped library path for a multi-step update needs a different attack setup that unrolls local training. It makes inversion harder and not easier, and it multiplies run time. It cannot be expected to help the plaintext view clear the floor |
| Larger batch per client | Dropped | It moves away from the floor, the opposite of the aim |
| More iterations (library default 24,000) | Dropped | At 8 records it gained +0.58 dB for six times the cost. Iterations stay at 4,000 as in P3 |
| The repository's own attack | Dropped | It was 4.4 to 5.3 dB below the floor in P3 in every view. B1c is not repeated |
| `max_weight` 16 collusion, exact (unquantised) aggregate, library default row | Dropped | P3 differences were 0.02, 0.12 and 0.58 dB. They cost 9 minutes each over 20 seeds. Because the exact aggregate is dropped, an aggregate difference cannot be split into mixing and quantisation here |
| Tuning the arm with 2 records separately | Dropped | Costs 6 minutes. The winners tuned at 4 records are reused at 2 records for every view. This is untested and listed below |

Two arms are run so that a failure of the 4 record gate does not leave the study without an interpretable arm. The 4 record arm stays closest to P3. No multiplicity correction is applied. Each gate is read on its own interval.

## Setup (fixed, changes from P3 marked)

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Data, model | As P3 (CIFAR-10 train split, real data only, small CNN of 25,578 parameters, one global model for all seeds). The model is the P3 checkpoint when its file is present (SHA-256 `66ef08e119ed94103d24384e1dc39044cafb45d5858c04cca99a1b1b4b264891`), and is retrained with the P3 recipe otherwise. Every JSON records which one was used and the hash |
| Seeds (changed) | Evaluation seeds 100 to 119, tuning seeds 9100 to 9102 |
| Clients, batch (changed) | N = 4 clients. Arm B4 has 4 records per client, arm B2 has 2, and the control has 1. One full batch SGD step at learning rate 0.15 |
| Records | For seed s, permutation `default_rng(1000 + s).permutation(...)` of the 30,000 images outside the training subset. The first 32 entries form four blocks of 8. Client c uses the first B entries of block c. Client 0 is the victim, so the victim images of B2 are the first two of B4, and the control record is the first. The public pool for the floor is every image except these 32, for all arms |
| Attacker knowledge | As P3. For the aggregate view the labels of all 4 B records |
| Views | As P3: plaintext (the victim's own update), aggregate (upstream SecAgg+ quantiser arithmetic, clipping range 8, quantisation range 2^22, `max_weight` 1000), collusion (aggregate minus the other three clients, rescaled) |
| Attack | `breaching` 0.1.2, shipped `invertinggradients` configuration, 4,000 iterations, total variation scale from the tuning rule, labels passed in, attack seed = seed + 1 (control: seed + 101) |
| Scoring | As P3: per image MSE, PSNR (data range 1) and SSIM, optimal one to one matching of reconstructions to originals over all images of the view, the victim rows reported |
| Floor | As P3: every victim record rebuilt as the class average image of the public pool, true label, no matching |

Rows per seed: control (1 record, plaintext), and for each arm plaintext, collusion and aggregate. That is 7 attacks of 4,000 iterations per seed. The control uses the plaintext winner of the tuning rule.

### Tuning rule (the only tuning)

Tuning seeds 9100 to 9102 at 4 records per client, for the plaintext, collusion and aggregate views, grid 0.05, 0.2, 0.8, 3.2, 4,000 iterations. The criterion is the highest mean PSNR over the tuning victim images (12 per view), ties to the earlier grid entry. The grid is not extended if the winner is at an edge, and the report says so. The winners are used for both arms and the control. Selection by PSNR may not be the best for SSIM, which is stated as a limitation. All tuning results are saved and reported.

## Predictions

Gain is the paired difference between the attack reconstruction and the floor reconstruction of the same victim image, averaged over the victim images of a seed, in PSNR (dB) and in SSIM. The unit of analysis is the seed. Intervals are 95% percentile bootstrap over the 20 evaluation seeds, 10,000 resamples, bootstrap seed 0. A rule that uses a limit is read at that limit and never at the point estimate. Paired differences are formed per seed before the bootstrap.

### Gates and reading order

A row is read only when its gate passed. A row that is not read is printed with the label NOT INTERPRETABLE and no PASS or FAIL. Its numbers are still stored and shown and may be described, but never as support for a statement about the aggregate or collusion view. The gates are fixed here.

| ID | Statement | Rule | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| K0 | Control, 1 record, plaintext, PSNR | Lower limit of gain at least +2.0 dB. If K0 fails every gate below is void and every row below is NOT INTERPRETABLE | 0.9 |
| G4P | Arm B4 plaintext clears the floor, PSNR | Lower limit of gain above +1.0 dB | 0.45 |
| G4S | Arm B4 plaintext clears the floor, SSIM | Lower limit of SSIM gain above +0.20 | 0.25 |
| G2P | Arm B2 plaintext clears the floor, PSNR | Lower limit of gain above +1.0 dB | 0.8 |
| G2S | Arm B2 plaintext clears the floor, SSIM | Lower limit of SSIM gain above +0.20 | 0.6 |

The rows C, Q, A and D of an arm and a metric are read only if the gate of that arm and that metric passed, and K0 passed. Gate G4P covers C4P, Q4P, A4P and D4P. Gate G4S covers C4S, Q4S, A4S and D4S, and so on for B2. A gate that fails is reported as FAIL, or as FAIL, underpowered when its point estimate is above the margin.

### Rows read after a gate (confidence is given that the gate passed)

Margins for PSNR are in dB, for SSIM in SSIM units, written as PSNR first and SSIM second.

| ID | Statement | Rule | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| C | Collusion keeps an attack above the floor | Lower limit of collusion gain above +1.0 and above +0.20 | 0.7 |
| Q | Quantisation costs little: collusion is close to plaintext | The whole interval of plaintext gain minus collusion gain lies inside plus and minus 1.0 and plus and minus 0.10 | 0.6 |
| A | The aggregate attack stays at or below the floor | Upper limit of aggregate gain at most +1.0 and at most +0.10 | 0.8 |
| D | Mixing costs the attacker | Lower limit of plaintext gain minus aggregate gain above +1.0 and above +0.10 | 0.8 |

Rule A alone is not read as a statement about the aggregate, because an attack that is worse than a class average image may be worse for reasons that have nothing to do with what the view hides. Rule D is the one that measures what the attacker loses. A passing D with a failing A (the aggregate attack still above the floor) is reported as exactly that.

### Row without a gate

| ID | Statement | Rule | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| T1 | Fewer records help the attack | On the first two victim images (the same images in both arms), plaintext PSNR gain of B2 minus B4 has a lower limit above 0 | 0.55 |

### Expected values (not rules)

Plaintext PSNR gain +3.0 dB at 2 records and +1.8 dB at 4 records. Plaintext SSIM gain +0.30 and +0.22. Collusion within 0.5 dB of plaintext. Aggregate PSNR gain about 0 dB at 2 records (8 mixed images, close to P3 plaintext with 8 records) and about -0.8 dB at 4 records (16 mixed images). The confidences above are judgments. The numbers of expected gain are the author's reading of P3 and nothing else.

### Rows reported without a rule

SSIM and PSNR gain with intervals for every row. The share of victim images where the attack MSE is below the floor MSE of the same image. The share of images with MSE below 0.05 (Wilson interval pooled over images, optimistic because the images of a seed are not independent). Wall time per row. The tuning table. The ratio of the observed seed standard deviation of each rule to the one assumed in the power section. When the PSNR and SSIM gains of a row disagree in sign the verdict text says so.

## Power of each null result

The power numbers come from `scripts/p3b_power_table.py`, which draws 20 seed level values from a normal distribution with the assumed mean and standard deviation, applies the exact decision rule with a 95% percentile bootstrap (2,000 resamples), and repeats 4,000 times with random seed 20261126. A cell has a Monte Carlo error of about 0.01. Assumed seed level standard deviations (PSNR, then SSIM): plaintext and collusion gain 1.8 and 0.12 at 4 records, 2.4 and 0.17 at 2 records, control 3.3. Aggregate gain 1.3 and 0.08 at 4 records, 1.6 and 0.11 at 2 records. Plaintext minus collusion 0.9 and 0.06 at 4 records, 1.2 and 0.08 at 2 records. Plaintext minus aggregate 2.2 and 0.13 at 4 records, 2.9 and 0.18 at 2 records. T1 2.4. If the observed seed level standard deviation of a rule is more than 1.5 times the assumed one, the power statement for that rule is void and the report says so.

Smallest true value that passes with probability 0.8 (lower limit rules), and the pass probability at the margin plus half an assumed standard deviation:

| Rule | Margin | True gain with power 0.8 | Power at margin plus half a standard deviation |
|---|---|---|---|
| K0 | +2.0 dB | 4.04 dB | 0.63 at 3.65 dB |
| G4P | +1.0 dB | 2.11 dB | 0.63 at 1.9 dB |
| G2P | +1.0 dB | 2.49 dB | 0.63 at 2.2 dB |
| G4S | +0.20 | 0.274 | 0.63 at 0.26 |
| G2S | +0.20 | 0.305 | 0.63 at 0.285 |
| D4P, D2P | +1.0 dB | 2.36 dB, 2.79 dB | 0.63 at 2.1 dB, 2.45 dB |
| D4S, D2S | +0.10 | 0.180, 0.211 | 0.63 at 0.165, 0.19 |
| T1 | 0 | 1.49 dB | 0.63 at 1.2 dB |

What a failed gate means. A gate that fails says that the plaintext gain was not shown to exceed the margin. At 4 records a true gain of +1.5 dB would clear G4P only about one time in five, so a failed G4P with a point estimate between +1.0 and +2.1 dB is labelled FAIL, underpowered and does not show that the attack is at the floor. The same holds for the other gates at their power-0.8 values above. The check cannot separate a true gain below the margin from one slightly above it. K0 is the positive control for the harness. A true control gain of 5.3 dB (P3 saw 5.5) passes K0 with probability 0.99.

Rule A (upper limit rules). A true aggregate gain of exactly 0 passes A with probability 0.93 (PSNR, 4 records), 0.81 (PSNR, 2 records), 1.00 (SSIM, 4 records) and 0.98 (SSIM, 2 records). A true gain at the margin passes with probability about 0.04, and a true gain of the margin plus half an assumed standard deviation or more passes with probability 0.00 in every cell. A failed A with a point estimate near zero at 2 records is therefore a power limit with a 19% chance of occurring even for a true gain of zero, and is reported that way. A passing A cannot rule out a true gain below the margin that is positive, for example +0.5 dB.

Rule Q (equivalence). Pass probability when the true difference between plaintext and collusion gain is 0, 0.25, 0.5 and 0.75 of the margin: PSNR at 4 records 1.00, 0.96, 0.72, 0.27 and at 2 records 0.92, 0.81, 0.49, 0.18. SSIM at 4 records 1.00, 1.00, 0.96, 0.49 and at 2 records 1.00, 0.99, 0.81, 0.32. So a passing Q rules out a true difference of 0.75 of the margin or more with probability 0.73 to 0.82 for PSNR and 0.51 to 0.68 for SSIM, and cannot rule out smaller differences. A failing Q at 2 records with a point estimate near zero is a power limit.

Every null result of this study (a failed gate, a passing A, a passing Q) covers only the views, the two batch sizes, the one model and the 4,000 iteration `breaching` configuration. None of them says anything about other attacks, other models or other batch sizes.

## Stopping rule

The run is fixed at 3 tuning seeds, 20 evaluation seeds (100 to 119), the 7 attacks per seed and the grid above. Verdicts are computed only when all 20 seeds are present. With fewer seeds the summary says "not the pre-registered size, no verdict". Resuming after a runtime drop is allowed as in P3. The script checks a settings hash and the model hash on every seed file and records the script SHA-256, device and runtime start time in each seed file, so that provenance per seed is evidenced. No replication is allowed. If every PSNR gate fails the study stops there and reports the gates, the control and the tuning table. No further arm is added after the first full run. A follow-up needs a new pre-registration.

## Reporting rules

1. Every row is reported as measured, next to the floor, with the interval, in both metrics. No row is dropped. A failed prediction is written as FAIL and not reworded.
2. A FAIL where the point estimate is on the passing side but the interval is not is labelled FAIL, underpowered. Other FAILs are labelled FAIL.
3. Thresholds, margins and grids are never changed after the first run that uses them. If one turns out to be badly chosen the original stays, a dated entry is added to the deviation log, and both outcomes are reported.
4. A reconstruction that is non-finite, or an all zero return of the library, is stored as returned with non-finite entries set to zero and counted in `invalid_reconstructions`. It is not retried.
5. The text never describes the aggregate result as privacy. It states what the attacks achieved. A row that is NOT INTERPRETABLE is never quoted as evidence that the aggregate or collusion view is safe or unsafe.
6. Results come from real CIFAR-10 on the Colab GPU run. CPU smoke output and any stand-in run never appear as results.
7. GPU runs are not bitwise reproducible. The JSON records device, torch and CUDA versions.
8. Findings about third party software are reported privately to the maintainers first. Nothing here is a statement about a defect in the library.

## Clauses that no script check covers

These stay untested or are read by hand.

1. The confidences, the expected values and the assumed standard deviations are judgments. The script reports the observed to assumed ratio but does not decide whether the judgment was sound.
2. The margins (+1.0 dB, +0.20, 1.0 dB and 0.10) are judgments. The SSIM margins were set after reading the P3 SSIM numbers.
3. That total variation winners tuned at 4 records transfer to 2 records, and that tuning by PSNR serves SSIM.
4. The counting argument about unknowns and equations and the reasons for dropping the levers are not tested.
5. The run time estimate (about 90 minutes) rests on 7 ms per iteration measured in P3 and is checked only by the run itself.
6. Whether the P3 checkpoint file on Drive is the one with the stated hash. The script checks the hash when it loads the file and records it. A retrained model is a different model from P3 and the report says which was used.
7. That seeds 100 to 119 and 9100 to 9102 were never used before. A test checks only that they do not overlap the P3 ranges.
8. The wording of the results document, including the rule that NOT INTERPRETABLE rows are not quoted as evidence. The release gate checks dashes, semicolons and banned words only.
9. That a normal seed level distribution describes the real one. The power table is a simulation of the rules and not of the data.
10. Which Colab runtime produced which seed is recorded by the script, but the script cannot prove that the recorded values are true.
11. The aggregate rows cannot be split into mixing and quantisation, because the exact aggregate row is dropped.
12. The comparison with P3 is context only. The seeds, the batch sizes and the grid differ.

## Limitations stated in advance

One dataset, one small CNN with 58% held out accuracy, one shared global model, one local step, known labels, known initial model and learning rate, 4 clients. The aggregate attacker is a joint oracle with all labels. Four total variation values were tried and the best may sit at an edge again. 4,000 iterations is shorter than the library default. SecAgg+ is simulated with the upstream quantiser arithmetic and not run over a network. PSNR rewards smooth images, SSIM rewards structure, and neither is a measure of what a person could recognise. A gain near zero does not mean that nothing leaked. The aggregate rows speak about 8 and 16 mixed images and not about larger groups.
