# v0.16 P3b results: a plaintext baseline above the floor, then collusion and aggregate rows

Status: measured. Pre-registration: `docs/preregistration/v0.16-p3b.md` (committed before the P3b script existed and before any attack call on real data, with deviation entries 1 to 8 written before the run and entries 9 to 11 written after it). Script: `scripts/p3b_breaching_cifar10.py`, SHA-256 `f2e575d882e0c1ea0fd33696af089410f0d4fb49479a44c26d22c2280c947a02`. Run date: 2026-11-26. Data: `reports/results/privacy/v0160/p3b_breaching/`.

This is a measurement of one library release (`breaching` 0.1.2), one small CNN, one local step on 4 or 2 records per client and the settings in the pre-registration. It is evidence about the attacks that were run. It is not a statement about privacy.

## Short answer

1. The control passed. With one record, `breaching` beats the label-only floor by +4.71 dB (interval +2.41 to +7.11). The seed to seed spread of this row was 1.69 times the one assumed in the power section, so the power statement of K0 is void. The pass itself stands, because its lower limit is above the margin.
2. With 4 records per client the plaintext gain was +0.74 dB (interval -0.03 to +1.57) in PSNR and +0.182 (interval +0.128 to +0.234) in SSIM. Both gates failed, so every C, Q, A and D row of the 4 record arm is NOT INTERPRETABLE and is not read.
3. With 2 records per client the plaintext gain was +2.79 dB (interval +1.47 to +4.12) in PSNR and +0.294 (interval +0.217 to +0.366) in SSIM. Both gates passed. At 2 records the attack beats the label-only floor in plaintext by more than the pre-registered margins, in both metrics.
4. Behind the passed 2 record gates: collusion keeps the attack above the floor (C2P and C2S passed), collusion is close to plaintext (Q2P and Q2S passed), and mixing costs the attacker (D2P and D2S passed). The aggregate view of 8 mixed images had a PSNR gain near zero, +0.02 dB (interval -0.83 to +1.01). A2P failed and is labelled underpowered by the pre-registered rule, because its point estimate is on the passing side of the margin while its upper limit is just above it. In SSIM the aggregate gain was +0.110 (interval +0.052 to +0.176) and A2S failed. So on SSIM the aggregate attack was not shown to be at or below the floor.
5. Fewer records helped the attacker (T1 passed): +2.08 dB (interval +0.96 to +3.22) on the first two victim images.
6. These are statements about the attacks that ran. None of them says anything about privacy. Rows that are NOT INTERPRETABLE are never quoted as evidence about the aggregate or collusion view.

## Floor

The label-only floor has mean PSNR 13.34 dB at 2 records and 13.12 dB at 4 records (control 13.40 dB). Its mean SSIM is 0.154 at 2 records and 0.154 at 4 records. The share of floor images with MSE below 0.05 is 0.45 at 2 records, so that cut separates attack from floor poorly, as in P3.

## Power and the observed spread

The observed seed level standard deviations were compared with the ones assumed in the power section. For K0 the ratio was 1.69 (observed 5.57 dB, assumed 3.3 dB), above the limit, so the power statement of K0 is void. A control that had failed would not have shown anything. It passed. For every other rule the ratio was between 0.61 and 1.37. No other power statement is void by the rule of the pre-registration.

What the failed rows mean:

* G4P failed with a point estimate of +0.74 dB, below the margin, so it is labelled FAIL and not underpowered. The power section says a true gain of +1.5 dB would clear it about one time in four, so this result does not show that the 4 record plaintext attack is at the floor. It says the attack was not shown to clear +1.0 dB.
* G4S failed with a point estimate of +0.182, below the margin of +0.20. The lower limit is +0.128. The same reading applies.
* A2P failed with the upper limit +1.008 dB just above the margin of +1.0 dB and the point estimate +0.02 dB. It is labelled FAIL, underpowered by the pre-registered rule. A true aggregate gain of 0 passed this rule about 81 times in 100 in the power table, which assumed a seed level standard deviation of 1.6 dB. The observed one was 2.19 dB (ratio 1.37, under the void limit), and at that spread the pass chance is lower, about 55 in 100 by a re-run of the power function. So a narrow miss of this kind is not unusual.
* A2S failed with the point estimate +0.110 above the margin of +0.10 and the upper limit +0.176. It is labelled FAIL.

Scorecard against the stated confidences. Held: K0 (0.9), G2P (0.8), G2S (0.6), C (0.7, both metrics at 2 records), Q (0.6, both metrics at 2 records), D (0.8, both metrics at 2 records), T1 (0.55). Failed: G4P (0.45), G4S (0.25), A2P (0.8), A2S (0.8). The failed predictions with confidence above 0.5 are A2P and A2S. Both were rule A at 2 records. The pre-registered statement that the aggregate attack stays at or below the floor did not hold in SSIM, and in PSNR it failed narrowly (point estimate near zero, upper limit just above the margin). Rows of the 4 record arm behind failed gates are not scored.

Expected values (not rules) against measured, plaintext gain: PSNR +2.79 dB at 2 records (expected +3.0) and +0.74 dB at 4 records (expected +1.8). SSIM +0.294 at 2 records (expected +0.30) and +0.182 at 4 records (expected +0.22). Aggregate PSNR gain at 2 records +0.02 dB (expected about 0). The 4 record aggregate expected value (-0.8 dB) is not compared because that row is NOT INTERPRETABLE.

## What the results say

* The harness works for the easy case. K0 passed and no reconstruction was invalid. The spread of the control (20 images in total, one per seed) is wide, so the size of its gain is imprecise even though its lower limit clears the margin.
* Batch size is the lever that changed the result between the arms. At 4 records per client the 4,000 iteration `breaching` attack gained +0.74 dB over the floor in plaintext, and at 2 records it gained +2.79 dB. The 4 record result is near the margin and was not shown to clear it. The 2 record result clears it with room, and T1 says the difference between the arms is above zero on the images that both arms share.
* At 2 records, the collusion attacker (4 clients, all but one known) reached the same gain as the plaintext attacker within the margin (Q2P and Q2S passed, plaintext minus collusion +0.03 dB (interval -0.33 to +0.39) in PSNR and -0.008 (interval -0.036 to +0.020) in SSIM). The pre-registered risk that quantisation would cost the attacker at small batches (deviation entry 7) was not seen at 2 records. Rule Q has limited power at this size (see the power section of the pre-registration), so a small cost would not have been detected.
* At 2 records, the aggregate view of 8 mixed images gave an attack with a PSNR gain of +0.02 dB, near the floor in PSNR, and +0.110 above it in SSIM. Plaintext minus aggregate was +2.77 dB (interval +2.00 to +3.51) in PSNR and +0.183 (interval +0.136 to +0.229) in SSIM (D2P and D2S passed). So the aggregate view of 8 mixed images cost the attacker in both metrics, and in SSIM the aggregate attack still sat above the floor. The aggregate rows cannot be split into mixing and quantisation, because the exact aggregate row was dropped (deviation entry 7).
* PSNR and SSIM do not tell the same story for the aggregate row at 2 records. PSNR gain is near zero and SSIM gain is above zero with an interval that excludes it. The pre-registration set both rules and both are reported. Neither is preferred.

## Limitations

One dataset, one small CNN (held-out accuracy 58%) shared by all seeds, one local step, known labels, known initial model and learning rate, 4 clients. The aggregate attacker is a joint oracle with all labels of the mixed images. Four total variation values were tried and the winner was not at the edge, but the winner tuned at 4 records was reused at 2 records and for the control without a test. 4,000 iterations is shorter than the library default. SecAgg+ is simulated with the upstream quantiser arithmetic and was not run through Flower. The 2 record aggregate has 8 mixed images and the 4 record aggregate has 16, and both are fewer than the 32 of P3, so the aggregate rows are not comparable with P3. The comparison with P3 is context only, since seeds, batch sizes and grid differ. No multiplicity correction was applied. Every null result covers only the views, the two batch sizes, the one model and the configuration above.

## Clauses that no script check covers

## Next steps

1. Decide whether the 2 record SSIM observation for the aggregate view (above the floor while PSNR is at the floor) is worth a pre-registered follow up with its own rule.
2. Separate mixing from quantisation for the aggregate view, which needs the exact unquantised aggregate row that was dropped from this study.
3. Decide whether a batch size between 4 and 2 (3 records) or a re-tuned total variation at 2 records is worth a new pre-registration. Neither was tested here.
4. Report an upstream note to the `breaching` maintainers only if a later reading of their issues shows something relevant. Nothing in this study is a statement about a defect in the library.
