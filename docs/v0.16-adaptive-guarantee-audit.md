# v0.16 T8c: the effective noise multiplier of the whole adaptive clipping mechanism in Flower

Status: measured, integrated in release 0.16.6 (not yet peer reviewed).
Pre-registration: `docs/preregistration/v0.16-adaptive-guarantee.md` (committed alone, as the first commit of the phase, before the harness existed and before any run).
Evidence: `reports/results/privacy/v0160/flower_adaptive_guarantee/flwr-1.27.0/`, `flwr-1.44.0/` and `cross_version/`.
Harness: `scripts/audit_flower_adaptive_guarantee.py`. Tests: `tests/privacy/test_audit_flower_adaptive_guarantee.py`.
Earlier phase: `docs/v0.16-adaptive-clipping-audit.md` measured the pieces. This phase measures what they give together.
Reference: Andrew, Thakkar, McMahan and Ramaswamy, arXiv 1905.03871.

## Summary

The harness runs the four adaptive and the four fixed clipping strategy classes of `flwr` (server side and client side, each in the legacy API and the Message API) with a `FedAvg` inner strategy and fake client replies. It simulates trajectories of the clip, 10 trajectories of 30 rounds in each of 24 cells per adaptive variant, with 100 clients, and records in every round the clip the clients used, the clip after the update, the noisy count and the model noise. It ran on `flwr` 1.27.0 and 1.44.0. All 640 rows per version held their pre-registered predictions, and the clip sequences of the two versions agree exactly. The four adaptive variants also agree exactly, which is expected because they share seeds and the arithmetic is the same.

No epsilon or delta is computed. Everything below is an effective noise multiplier or a ratio of one to the configured value, under the assumptions listed in the next section.

1. What Flower sends for the count. The bit is 0 or 1 with no shift. The client mods send a Python `bool` (legacy) or an `int` (Message API) with values in {0, 1}, and the bit is 1 when the update is clipped. The strategy passes the plain number of set bits to the noise draw, with the stored `clipped_count_stddev` as the scale (rows G0a to G0c). Moving one client from inside to outside the clip changes that number by exactly 1 in all 100 cases, and leaving one reply out changes it by that reply's bit, 0 or 1 (G0d, G0e). So the sensitivity of the released count is 1 under add or remove. The paper's centred bit (b - 1/2) has magnitude 1/2, as read once.
2. The clip the clients used. The clients use the value of `clipping_norm` from before the round, and for the client side classes it is the value in the config that they receive (row G1, relative differences at most 1e-6, float32). The model noise is scaled with the clip after the round's update. So the effective multiplier of the model sum in round t is z_delta times C(t+1) / C(t), where z_delta is the stored multiplier and C(t) is the clip the clients used. The paper reading, which uses the clip of the round, gives z_delta in every round (rows G2a, G4).
3. At the default learning rate 0.2 and the default count noise (sigma_b 5.0, configured z 1.0, 100 clients) with every update clipped, the cell mean of that ratio is 0.9054 and the minimum and maximum over 300 rounds are 0.873 and 0.940. The effective multiplier on the model sum is 0.910 of the configured z. At learning rate 1.0 the ratio is 0.606 and at 0.05 it is 0.975. When no update is clipped the ratio is above 1 (the noiseless value is exp(lr / 2), 1.105 at the default).
4. The composed multiplier of the model sum and the count, with the count sensitivity of 1 that Flower sends, is 0.8952 of the configured z in the same cell (0.864 to 0.928 over rounds). With the paper's count sensitivity of 1/2 applied to the same round values it is 0.9062. If the clip noise ratio were 1 the paper relation would give exactly 1, and Flower's count would give 0.98533 at the default sigma_b of 5.0. At the lowest allowed count noise (sigma_b 0.6, just above z / 2) the Flower count gives 0.56949 of the configured z.
5. Fixed clipping classes keep the clip, release no count, and have an effective multiplier of exactly the configured value in every round (rows G5a, G5b).

## Assumptions behind the composed numbers

These are repeated beside every composed result.

1. Gaussian releases and full participation of all n = 100 clients with equal weights, so no subsampling amplification is used.
2. The per user contribution is bounded in norm as in the paper's proof, with the model part bounded by the clip the clients used and the count part bounded by s_b. Flower reading: s_b = 1, because the bit is 0 or 1 and not shifted. Paper reading: s_b = 1/2, the centred bit.
3. In a round the model sum and the count are treated as one joint Gaussian release, so the composed multiplier is Z = (zeta^-2 + (sigma_b / s_b)^-2)^(-1/2), with zeta the multiplier of the model sum.
4. Rounds are composed by adding precisions. The equivalent constant multiplier Z_eq of a trajectory is (mean of Z^-2 over rounds)^(-1/2). It describes the realised trajectory. It is not a bound over trajectories, because the noise scale of a round depends on the noisy count of the same round.
5. If the sensitivity of the count is taken as the range of the bit under replacement of a user, the range is 1 for both the paper's centred bit and Flower's bit. The comparison then depends on the sensitivity taken for the model sum (2 times the clip under replacement), which this harness does not measure. Both Z_flower and Z_paper are reported so that either reading can be applied. No claim is made about which convention Flower intends.
6. The paper's reasons for the factor 2 in (2 sigma_b) are not given in the paper in words. The reading that it comes from the centred bit is an inference and is unconfirmed.

## Setup

256 float32 parameters, global model zero, `FedAvg` with `num_examples` of 1. Updates lie along the first coordinate, and the other 255 coordinates carry the model noise alone. The 100 update norms are fixed at 100 evenly spaced values from 0.5 to 1.5. The global model is not carried between rounds, so a trajectory is a trajectory of the clip under a fixed set of norms.

Adaptive grid per variant: sigma_b in 0.6, 2.5, 5.0, 10.0, `clip_norm_lr` in 0.05, 0.2, 1.0, start clip 0.1 (below every norm, so every update is clipped) and 1.0 (the median). Configured z is 1.0 and the target quantile 0.5. 10 trajectories of 30 rounds per cell, seeds `20261201 + 100000 a + 10000 b + 1000 c + j` with the same seed for every variant and version. Fixed grid per variant: z in 0.5, 1.0, 2.0, clip in 0.1, 1.0, 4 trajectories of 10 rounds. For the client side adaptive classes the trajectory bits come from Flower's own `adaptive_clip_inputs_inplace`, and rows G0 and G1 run the real client mods of both APIs and compare their bits. A wrapper around `np.random.normal` records the count draw and forwards to the original function.

Both full runs took 493 seconds on one core.

### Fixed clipping classes (G5)

For all four fixed classes and both versions: the clip is unchanged by the call, the count draw never happens, the effective multiplier of the model sum is the configured z (largest relative difference 0 at the 1e-12 tolerance), and the composed multiplier is the configured z because no count is released. The pooled root mean square of the model noise standardised by z times the clip over n is 0.9910 over 61,200 values (standard error 0.29 percent, tolerance 1.5 percent). The four fixed classes gave the same pooled value because they share seeds.

### Measured model noise against the stored scale (G4)

The pooled root mean square of the model noise, standardised by the stored multiplier times the clip after the update over n, is 1 within the tolerance for every variant, version and learning rate. For V1 on 1.27.0: 0.9983, 1.0016 and 1.0019 over all cells at lr 0.05, 0.2 and 1.0, and 0.9994, 1.0013 and 1.0016 over the start 0.1 cells (standard errors 0.09 and 0.13 percent). If the noise were scaled with the clip before the update, the start 0.1 cells would give 1.0253, 1.1050 and 1.656, so the check separates the two readings at every learning rate.

### Agreement between variants and versions (G7)

Clip sequences of V2, V3 and V4 agree with V1 within 1e-9 relative in every cell (the difference is 0), and the sequences of 1.44.0 agree with those of 1.27.0 in all 96 cells compared (maximum relative difference 0.0). Because every variant and version used the same seeds, this shows that the arithmetic is the same and is not independent confirmation.

## Where the measurement differs from the paper reading

Each item gives the configuration that would reach the paper reading, or says that none was found.

1. Noise scale against the clip used (G2, G4). The effective multiplier of the model sum is z_delta times C(t+1) / C(t), and the paper reading is z_delta. Configuration needed: none found. The ratio is 1 only when the clip does not move in the round, which in the adaptive classes needs the fraction of set bits to equal the target quantile. The fixed classes have the ratio 1.
2. Count sensitivity (G0, G3). Flower's count is the plain sum of 0 or 1 bits, with sensitivity 1 under add or remove. The paper's centred bit has magnitude 1/2. With the paper relation z_delta = (z^-2 - (2 sigma_b)^-2)^(-1/2) the composed multiplier with Flower's count is (z^-2 + 0.75 sigma_b^-2)^(-1/2), below z. Configuration needed to reach the paper reading under this convention: none found among the constructor arguments, because the stored z_delta uses the factor 2 and the count draw has no shift argument. Under the range convention of assumption 5 the two counts have the same range, and this item then has no reading until the model sum's sensitivity is fixed.
3. The two items add up: in the default start 0.1 cell (sigma_b 5.0, lr 0.2) the clip ratio alone puts the multiplier of the model sum at 0.9099 of the configured z, and the count with sensitivity 1 lowers the composed value to 0.8952.

## Limits

1. Two releases only, 1.27.0 and 1.44.0, run with the default Python 3.13. The 1.44.0 fixed clipping classes have an optional `accountant` argument. It was not used. Other releases were not run.
2. All variants and both versions used the same seeds, so agreement between them is expected and is not independent evidence.
3. Fake client replies, one small synthetic parameter vector, a fixed set of 100 update norms, 100 clients, z = 1.0 in the trajectory grid, and 30 rounds. No training, no real data, no deployment, and no feedback from the noisy model to the update norms.
4. The grid has a single value of z in the trajectories. The dependence on z comes from the constructor grid (G6) only and not from trajectories.
5. Full participation and equal weights only. Partial participation and unequal weights are in the first phase of v0.16.
6. The composed numbers depend on the assumptions listed above. In particular they assume the paper's per user contribution convention and a joint Gaussian release in each round. The equivalent constant multiplier is a description of realised trajectories and not a bound for the mechanism over all trajectories. No subsampling and no accountant were used and no epsilon or delta is reported.
7. The paper's centring of the bit and the relation z_delta were read from a summarising fetch of the arXiv PDF in this phase and in the earlier phase. This was not a line by line comparison with the PDF. The paper's reason for the factor 2 is not stated in words and the reading used here is an inference.
8. The client side trajectories take their bits from Flower's `adaptive_clip_inputs_inplace`, which is the function the mods call. The mods themselves were run in rows G0 and G1.
9. The harness was written after the pre-registration commit in the same working session. The predictions of G2 follow from the earlier phase's results and are confirmatory in kind. The numbers in the pre-registered table were computed from the formulas before the harness existed.
10. One smoke run in quick mode was done before the full runs, as listed in the deviation log.

## Reproduce

```bash
python3 -I scripts/audit_flower_adaptive_guarantee.py --out out/            # about 8 minutes, 1.27.0
PYTHONPATH=/tmp/flwr44 python3 scripts/audit_flower_adaptive_guarantee.py --out out44/   # 1.44.0
python3 -I scripts/audit_flower_adaptive_guarantee.py --compare out/ out44/ --out cmp/
python3 scripts/audit_flower_adaptive_guarantee.py --out out/ --quick       # about 70 seconds
pytest tests/privacy/test_audit_flower_adaptive_guarantee.py
```

Quick mode uses 2 trajectories of 10 rounds. Row G4 can miss its tolerance in quick mode, because the standard error is about 0.35 percent.
