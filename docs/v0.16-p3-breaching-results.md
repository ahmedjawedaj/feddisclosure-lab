# v0.16 P3 results: breaching as the primary inversion attack on CIFAR-10

Status: measured. Pre-registration: `docs/preregistration/v0.16-p3.md` (committed before any tuning or evaluation run). Script: `scripts/p3_breaching_cifar10.py`, SHA-256 `26b2b3a573db916663e18080bb3805259c7939f1b0bae8daa5e3d1c456517124`. Run date: 2026-11-24. Data: `reports/results/privacy/v0160/p3_breaching/`.

This is a measurement of one library release (`breaching` 0.1.2), one small CNN, one local step on 8 records per client and the settings in the pre-registration. It is evidence about the attacks that were run. It is not a statement about privacy.

## Short answer

1. The control passed. With a batch of one record, `breaching` beats the label-only floor by +5.50 dB (interval +4.71 to +6.31) and beats the floor on 96% of images. The harness and the configuration work when the problem is easy.
2. With a batch of 8 records, `breaching` did not beat the label-only floor in the plaintext view. Gain over the floor was -0.12 dB (interval -0.72 to +0.42). R1 failed. The pre-registered prediction that plaintext sits clearly above the floor was wrong for this setup.
3. Collusion (n-1) also sits at the floor: -0.01 dB (interval -0.64 to +0.56). R3 failed. Quantisation did not visibly cost the attacker anything, because the attacker had nothing to lose over the floor to begin with (O1 failed).
4. The SecAgg+ aggregate view sits below the floor: -1.47 dB (interval -1.83 to -1.13). R2 passed but is labelled vacuous by the pre-registered rule, because the upper limit is below -1.0 dB.
5. The repository's own attack was far weaker than `breaching` in every view (gain -4.4 to -5.3 dB, B1c ratio 2.59). The B1 pattern from v0.15 repeated on CIFAR-10 with equal tuning effort.
6. Because the plaintext view itself did not clear the floor, this experiment cannot tell the aggregate view apart from the plaintext view in PSNR. It neither supports nor weakens the earlier aggregate-at-floor statements. It does not show that the aggregate is safe.

## Floor

The label-only floor (every victim record rebuilt as the class-average image of the public pool) has mean PSNR 13.07 dB and mean MSE 0.058. Half of the floor images already pass the v0.14 "recognisable" cut of MSE below 0.05. That cut therefore separates attack from floor poorly on CIFAR-10 and carries no pass rule.

Every FAIL has its point estimate on the failing side, so none is labelled "underpowered". For R1 and R3 the upper limits (+0.42 and +0.56) are below the +1.0 dB margin, so a true gain of +1 dB or more is rejected for these rows.

The observed seed-level standard deviations of gain are 1.32 dB (plaintext), 0.81 dB (aggregate) and 1.41 dB (collusion). All are below the 3 dB limit at which the pre-registered power statements become void, and close to the 1.5 dB that was assumed. The power statements stand.

Scorecard against the stated confidences: K0 (0.8) held, R1 (0.6) failed, R2 (0.8) held, R3 (0.4) failed, R1r (0.5) failed, R2r (0.9) held, R3r (0.3) failed, B1c (0.4) failed, O1 (0.6) failed, O2 (0.8) held. The failed predictions with confidence above 0.5 are R1 and O1.

## What the results say

* The setup is not broken. K0 passed by a wide margin, 96% of control images beat the floor, no reconstruction was invalid, and the mean tuning PSNR of the plaintext view was already 12.77 dB at the selected setting, close to the evaluation floor of 13.07 dB, before any evaluation seed was run.
* In PSNR, a class-average image is a strong prior on natural images, and a batch of 8 records through one gradient step gave the 4,000 iteration `breaching` attack no measurable edge over it. This matches the reasoning given for the R1 confidence in the pre-registration, which was less than certain for this reason.
* The aggregate view is 1.5 dB below the floor, and the exact unquantised aggregate is 1.6 dB below it. So the gap between the aggregate and the collusion view (O2) is caused by mixing 32 images into one gradient and not by quantisation. Collusion at max_weight 16 is the same as at 1000.
* The repository attack was weaker than `breaching` by 2.9 to 5.2 dB in all three views, with the same four-point tuning grid. The older B1 finding is therefore not explained by uneven tuning effort alone.

## Exploratory observations (not pre-registered, no rule attached)

* SSIM and PSNR disagree for the plaintext and collusion rows. SSIM gain is positive with intervals that exclude zero (plaintext +0.116, collusion +0.124, max_weight 16 +0.110), and PSNR gain is about zero. For the aggregate rows SSIM gain is near zero (-0.007 and -0.021, intervals include zero). On SSIM the plaintext and collusion views look different from the aggregate view. This is a post hoc reading of one extra metric and should be tested with a pre-registered rule before it is relied on.
* The library default configuration (24,000 iterations, total variation 0.2) gave +0.46 dB (interval -0.26 to +1.14), higher than the 4,000 iteration row but still not clearly above the floor. It was run on the plaintext view only.
* The tuning rule picked the largest total variation value in the grid (0.2) for all three `breaching` views, and the repo attack's best values were 0.5 (plaintext, aggregate, also the grid edge) and 0.1 (collusion). The grids were fixed in advance and were not changed. A larger value than 0.2 may help `breaching`, which means the attack in this study may be somewhat under-tuned at the grid edge. This is untested.

## Limitations

One dataset, one small CNN (held-out accuracy 58%) shared by all seeds, one local step on 8 records per client, known labels, known initial model and learning rate, 4 clients. The aggregate attacker is a joint oracle with all 32 labels. Four total variation values were tried per view, with the best at the edge of the grid for `breaching`. 4,000 iterations is shorter than the library default. SecAgg+ is simulated with the upstream quantiser arithmetic. PSNR rewards smooth images and the class average is smooth, so a gain near zero does not mean that nothing leaked, and the SSIM rows suggest that something may be present in the plaintext and collusion views. Intervals use seeds as units, but the model is shared, so model to model variance is not included. Two runtimes produced the 20 seeds.
