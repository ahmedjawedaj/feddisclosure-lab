# v0.16.11: noise and sensitivity delivered by the distributed DP aggregator of TensorFlow Federated 0.87.0, measured against its configuration

Status: measured on `tensorflow-federated` 0.87.0, integrated in release 0.16.11 (not yet peer reviewed).
Pre-registration: `docs/preregistration/v0.16-tff-ddp.md` (commit `b32e31c`, committed alone before the script was written and before any call with noise in this phase).
Evidence: `reports/results/privacy/v0160/tff_ddp/tff-0.87.0/tff_ddp_audit.json`.
Script: `scripts/audit_tff_ddp.py`. Tests: `tests/privacy/test_audit_tff_ddp.py`.
Follows: the Gaussian aggregators of the same release line in `docs/v0.16-tff-dp-audit.md`, whose method this measurement reuses for a different mechanism.

## Summary

The script runs the real `DistributedDpSumFactory` (Skellam and discrete Gaussian mechanisms, discrete Fourier and Hadamard rotations, modular clipping, adaptive clip) through `process.next` in TFF's C++ runtime. It asks what noise and what sensitivity come out for a stated configuration, and whether the documentation matches the code. Every prediction was computed by helper functions that do not call TFF and was committed before any call that produced noise.

8 of 12 rows held and 194 of 199 scripted checks held. Five checks failed. Three were statistical and held in the single replication that the pre-registered rule allows, so no mismatch is called for them. Two stand as failed predictions: the noise-free output norm at 20 clients (an effect of the modular range that the prediction did not take into account) and row J, whose call did not run. Of the 199 checks, one in row V only records that the test execution context is used and cannot fail.

1. Delivered central noise. For 10 clients and noise multiplier 1 the root mean square of the delivered noise over `noise_multiplier * l2_clip` was 1.00121 (Skellam, dft), 0.99871 (Skellam, hd), 1.00104 (discrete Gaussian, dft) and 1.00196 (discrete Gaussian, hd). Each is within 0.7% of its prediction (ratios to prediction 1.00133, 0.99883, 1.00103 and 1.00195). The noise is normal by skewness and kurtosis (at most 0.0037 and 0.0153 in absolute value).
2. Documentation against code. The documentation says that the local noise standard deviation is the central one divided by `expected_clients_per_round`. The code divides by the square root of that number, and the factory returns a sum. The delivered ratio to `noise_multiplier * l2_clip` was 0.99641 for 4 clients and 1.00015 for 25 clients (predicted 0.99983 and 0.99998). The reading of the sentence would have given 1.99 and 5.00.
3. Fewer or more clients than expected. With 5 and 20 clients reporting against 10 expected, the noise over `noise_multiplier * l2_clip` was 0.70807 and 1.40720 for Skellam (0.70547 and 1.40360 for the discrete Gaussian), which follows the square root of m over n. The sensitivity of one client stayed at 1.0 times the clip, so the delivered noise multiplier was 0.708 and 1.407 for the configured 1. The configured multiplier holds only when the expected number of clients reports.
4. Sensitivity. A client above the clip changes the output by 1.00000 times the clip at 16 bits, 1.00708 at 8 bits and 1.45129 at 5 bits (mean of 80 calls, predicted 1.00000, 1.00765 and 1.4540). The sensitivity is above the clip in every one of the 80 calls at 8 and 5 bits, because the stochastic rounding adds to the norm after the clip. The deterministic bounds `clip + sqrt(D) / scale` were 1.00119, 1.30434 and 3.6341, and no call exceeded them (largest 1.00002, 1.01349 and 1.50549). A `beta` of exp(-0.5) changed the mean by less than 0.005.
5. Bit width. The Skellam noise stayed at `noise_multiplier * l2_clip` for 4, 5, 8 and 12 bits (0.99714, 1.00494, 0.99952 and 1.00175). The discrete Gaussian noise was 2.41687, 1.67157, 1.08342 and 1.00292 times that value, as predicted from the integer scale (2.42095, 1.67489, 1.08361 and 1.00270). The excess is noise above the configured level. With a dense input of norm 0.99 the error grows by the randomized rounding to 1.02155 times the configured noise at 5 bits and 1.06457 at 4 bits (predicted 1.0209 and 1.0636).
6. Modular wrap. With `modclip_prob` 0.3 and 0.7 the noise was 0.60842 and 0.22789, equal to the wrap model (0.60814 and 0.22783) and not to the saturating value (0.74456 and 0.35147). With 16 and 64 clients against 4 expected at 8 bits, inputs of true sum norm 16 and 64 came out with norm 8.96 and 9.00 (wrap 8.96 and 8.99, clip 11.30 and 14.53), and the call returned without an error. The constructor limits on `bits` and `modclip_prob` were as predicted for the seven client counts tried.
7. Adaptive clip. The noise follows the clip that the clients used in the round (pooled ratios 0.9948 to 1.0039 over six rounds) and the clip then falls by `exp(-0.1)` at most per round (steps 0.9048 to 0.9225). The scale times the clip is constant along a chain and the local noise tracks the clip exactly in every round. After six rounds the clip was 0.616 of the initial one on average.
8. Accounting surface. After one round the state holds an `UnsupportedDpEvent` with no fields, for both mechanisms and for the adaptive configuration. The initial state raises `ExtractingDpEventFromInitialStateError`. No state or measurement name contains "epsilon", "delta" or "noise_multiplier" and no name contains "epsilon" in eight source files outside docstrings and comments. The class docstring has a TODO about exact privacy accounting.
9. Randomness. Two calls from one state are uncorrelated (correlation 0.00507 and 0.00200, lag-1 autocorrelation 0.00572 and -0.00206 for Skellam and the discrete Gaussian). Two fresh processes that call `tf.random.set_seed(12345)` and `numpy.random.seed(12345)` give different outputs for both mechanisms, so the Python global seed does not control the noise.
10. Repeated noise between clients, an open observation. The per-call RMS of Skellam in A1 had a standard deviation of 0.0218 in the first run (window 0.0116 to 0.0197) and one call at 1.123. The replication was inside the window (0.0172) and had one call at 1.0998 in a different configuration. The pre-registered rule makes the replication decisive, so this is not called a mismatch. An exploratory scratch run after the evidence (not part of the evidence file and not backed by the JSON) made 500 zero-input calls per mechanism. Seven of the 500 Skellam calls had an RMS between 1.094 and 1.128, against 0 of 500 for the discrete Gaussian (largest 1.046). Two clients that draw the same noise would give about 1.095, which the seven values are consistent with. The Skellam seeds come from a clock in microseconds. The cause was not isolated.
11. Implied epsilon, labelled. Each entry of row I is a Gaussian approximation conditional on the measured numbers, with add or remove adjacency, and not a guarantee. The table is below.
12. `tff.learning.ddp_secure_aggregator(1.0, 20)` constructs and initializes, and a call of `next` did not run in either execution context, so row J has no measured noise.
13. Runtime. A call of `next` fails in TFF's default local C++ execution context with the gRPC error "Cannot embed a federated value" for every configuration tried. Every row used the test C++ execution context, which runs the same call. The whole script took 375.1 seconds.

These are measurements of one TFF version in stated configurations. They say nothing about the guarantee of a trained run, other aggregators or other versions. The TFF documentation read states no adjacency relation.

## Setup

`tensorflow-federated` 0.87.0 in a Python 3.11 virtual environment with `tensorflow` 2.14.1, `tensorflow-privacy` 0.9.0, `dp-accounting` 0.4.3, `numpy` 1.25.2, `jax` 0.4.14, `jaxlib` 0.4.14 and `scipy` 1.9.3 (row V confirms the versions). The class is not exported by `tff.aggregators` in this release and was imported from `tensorflow_federated.python.aggregators.distributed_dp`. The CI environment of this repository has no TFF, so the tests that call it skip there. TFF runs the aggregation in a C++ worker process that it starts, and the script ends it when it finishes.

Every row uses one float32 tensor of 2048 coordinates, so no padding or slicing happens for either rotation. Noise rows send zero vectors, so the output is the noise, and the statistic is the root mean square over all coordinates of all calls with relative standard error 1 over the square root of 2N. The secure sum is a plain sum in this runtime, so the wrap comes from the modular clipping components and a real secure aggregation field was not run. Sensitivity rows use a noise multiplier of 0 and a fresh initial state per call, so that rotation seeds vary. Adjacency is not stated by the documentation read. The reading used is add or remove one client.

## Power of each null result

For an RMS check the decision threshold is a fixed tolerance, so a true deviation d is found with 80% power when d is at least the tolerance plus 0.8416 standard errors. The script recomputed the first two figures and they matched the pre-registered ones. The others are the pre-registered figures and were not recomputed.

| Null result | N | Standard error | Decision threshold | Smallest deviation found with 80% power |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A1 RMS ratios | 245,760 | 0.1426% | 0.7% | 0.820% |
| A2, B, C, E2 RMS ratios | 163,840 | 0.1747% | 0.7% | 0.847% |
| A3 RMS ratios (absolute) | 163,840 | 0.1747% | 0.8% | 0.947% |
| F RMS ratios per round | 40,960 | 0.3492% | 1.5% | 1.79% |
| D mean norm, 8 bits | 80 | 0.000316 | 0.0012 | 0.00147 |
| D mean norm, 5 bits | 80 | 0.00247 | 0.02 | 0.0221 |
| E3 mean norm, 64 and 16 clients | 20 | about 0.02 and 0.03 | 0.2 and 0.4 | 0.22 and 0.43 |
| H correlations | 40,960 | 0.00494 | 0.0198 | 0.0239 |
| H seed digests | two processes | none | equality of digests | any difference |
| Exact checks | one run each | float32 rounding below 1e-6 | stated relative tolerance | any difference above the tolerance on the inputs tried |

The evidence file reports 0.01692 as the 80% power point of the H correlations. That number comes from a different count of values than the pre-registered 0.0239, and the pre-registered figure is the one used here. All four measured correlations were below 0.006, so no conclusion depends on the difference.

The A1 dispersion check can find a share of about 2.3% of calls that carry a repeated-noise pair with 80% power, and the exploratory run after the evidence saw about 1.4% for Skellam. A share of that size is below the power of the pre-registered check, which is why the first run failed and the replication did not.

Effects that matter for the readings are far above these limits. The two readings of the documentation sentence differ by a factor of the square root of n (2.0 at n 4). The arrived-count reading differs by 41% at 5 clients. The integer scale at 8 bits changes the discrete Gaussian noise by 8.4%. A saturating field against a wrapping one differs by 22% at `modclip_prob` 0.3. The positive controls (B check 2, C check 2, E2, F check 2, H check 3) each registered a deviation of that size for the alternative reading and not for TFF's.

What a pass covers. Zero update vectors for the noise rows, one dense direction per row for the rounding and sensitivity rows, the stated n, m, bits and `modclip_prob`, 2048 coordinates in one float32 tensor, one TFF version, one platform and the test C++ runtime. It does not cover other dimensions, integer inputs, several tensors, other norms, a trained model, a real secure aggregation protocol, client sampling, composition over rounds or any claim about privacy.

## Clauses that no script check covers

These are read or judged by hand and are listed in the pre-registration.

1. The statements under "What was already known" that describe the source code, the probe output, the scratch arithmetic and the install route.
2. The eight claimed behaviours, copied by hand from the sources and shortened. The text checks of B, F and G test only the quoted fragments.
3. The paper reading (the scale rule, the conditional rounding bound, the share of variance per client, the two mechanism definitions).
4. The Gaussian model of rotated coordinates used for the rounding variance in A3, D and E3, and the exact table of a sum of discrete Gaussian variables used in place of the TFP sampler.
5. That the two rotations are orthonormal. The rows test it only through their ratios.
6. That the secure sum is a plain sum in TFF's local runtime and that the wrap comes from the modular clipping components.
7. That the documentation read states no adjacency relation.
8. That the scan for "epsilon" names covers eight files only.
9. The false failure probability estimate and the "What a pass covers" paragraph.
10. That the C++ worker process runs the same TensorFlow kernels as the Python side.
11. The implied epsilon table as a Gaussian approximation of a discrete mechanism, and the choice of add or remove adjacency.
12. The wording of the README and this document.
13. The scratch diagnostics of deviation log items 9 and 11. Their numbers are not in the evidence file.

## Differences from the Gaussian aggregators of the earlier TFF release

By hand reading, for the configurations tried, and not a statement about intent.

| Question | `DifferentiallyPrivateFactory` (v0.16.9) | `DistributedDpSumFactory` (this release) |
|---|---|---|
| Fewer or more clients than configured | noise and sensitivity both use the configured count, so the multiplier stays at z | the local noise is set for the configured count and the summed noise follows the square root of the reporting count, so the delivered multiplier is z times the square root of m over n |
| Noise level against the configuration | within 0.21% of `z * C / n` | within 0.7% for Skellam at 16 bits, and 8.4% above for the discrete Gaussian at 8 bits, 67% above at 5 bits and 142% above at 4 bits |
| Sensitivity of an update above the clip | exactly the clip | the clip at 16 bits, 0.7% above at 8 bits and 45% above at 5 bits, because of rounding |
| Accounting | a per-round Gaussian event | an `UnsupportedDpEvent` with no fields |
| Seed control | the Python global seed does not control the noise | the same |

## Limits

1. One TFF version (0.87.0). Earlier releases were not run.
2. The aggregation ran in the test C++ runtime on CPU. The default local runtime did not run a call, and a real secure aggregation protocol was not run.
3. The noise rows use zero updates and the sensitivity rows use one dense direction. A trained model with data dependent norms was not run.
4. One dimension (2048) and one tensor. Padding, slicing and several tensors were not measured.
5. `ddp_secure_aggregator` did not run a call, so the wrapper, its defaults (20 bits, Hadamard rotation, zeroing, adaptive clip) and the scale cap are untested.
6. No adjacency relation is documented, and no epsilon was computed as a guarantee. A delivered multiplier is a statement about the noise. The sampling of clients, the composition over rounds and the adjacency convention stay with the user.
7. The noise could not be seeded, so the saved evidence is one run with one replication for the rows that needed it.
8. Row I uses a Gaussian approximation of a discrete mechanism and ignores wrap and the L1 term.
9. The repeated-noise observation (item 10 of the summary) is from a scratch run outside the evidence. A pre-registered measurement of its rate and cause was not made.
10. The H threshold in the script was stricter than the pre-registered one, and the evidence file's 80% power figure for H differs from the pre-registered one.

## Reproduce

```bash
uv venv --python 3.11 tff_venv
uv pip install --python tff_venv/bin/python --find-links https://storage.googleapis.com/jax-releases/jax_releases.html tensorflow-federated==0.87.0
tff_venv/bin/python scripts/audit_tff_ddp.py               # about 6 minutes, writes the evidence file
tff_venv/bin/python scripts/audit_tff_ddp.py --quick --out out/   # about 3 minutes, not evidence
tff_venv/bin/python -m pytest tests/privacy/test_audit_tff_ddp.py
```
