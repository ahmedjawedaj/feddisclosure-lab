Pre-registration: `docs/preregistration/v0.15.md`. Thresholds were not changed. Results are in
`reports/results/privacy/v0150/fashion_mnist/` and the verdict in `reports/results/privacy/v0150/prereg_check_fashion.json`.

**X1 is recorded as FAIL.** Plaintext and the aggregate view meet their rules. n-1 collusion at the primary
setting reaches +9.4 points over the floor, 0.6 points short of the required +10. The same rule at a finer SecAgg+ weight ratio
passes. Both outcomes are reported below, the verdict stays FAIL. The verdict is the local 20-seed result at the primary setting. X1 is close to a coin flip (see the results section), and the tuning that fed it has limits that are listed below.

## Model and protocol

Small CNN: two 3x3 convolutions (8 and 16 channels) with ReLU and max pooling, then one linear layer. 9,098 parameters.
The architecture was fixed before any tuning or evaluation run.

The protocol is the digits view study (`docs/v0.15-scaling.md`) on 28x28 images. 4 clients hold 8 distinct images each and take one full-batch SGD step
(learning rate 0.15) from a shared initial model. Client 0 is the victim. The attacker knows labels, learning rate and initial model.
Per-image MSE below 0.05 on pixels in [0, 1] is "recognisable". Views:

- plaintext: the victim update.
- n-1 collusion: the SecAgg+ aggregate (upstream quantiser, clipping range 8, quantisation range 2^22) minus the other clients' updates.
- aggregate only: the SecAgg+ aggregate with joint-oracle labels, matching all 32 images at once.
- label floor: every victim record rebuilt as the class-average image of the 59,968 public images that no client holds.

The attack is the repository's gradient matching, generalised to the CNN (`fashion_mnist_study.invert`). It sees only the initial model, the observed delta, the labels and the learning rate.
The primary SecAgg+ `max_weight` is 1000. It is the upstream default and the README headline setting, and the pre-registration does not name it. `tune_one` hardcodes it, and WP9 and WP10 landed in one commit, so "fixed before the run" is not separately timestamped. The choice hurts X1, because at `max_weight` 16 the collusion part passes. `max_weight` 16 is a secondary setting.

**Tuning.** Attack settings (iterations, step size, total-variation weight) were chosen on tuning seeds 9000 to 9002 (24 victim images), separately for the individual,
collusion and aggregate views (`tuning.json`, 35 settings in total). Chosen: 3000 iterations and step 0.1 for all three,
total-variation weight 0.1 (plaintext), 0.5 (collusion) and 0.05 (aggregate). Heavy smoothing is a prior, not information from the update, which is why every number is shown next to the label floor.
What the tuning does and does not cover:

- The criterion was lowest mean victim MSE, not recognisable rate. For the collusion view the two disagree. At total-variation weight 0.2 the recognisable rate on the tuning images was 83.3% and the mean MSE was 0.0395. At 0.5 the rate was 70.8% and the mean MSE was 0.0382. MSE picked 0.5, a rate criterion would have picked 0.2. The evaluation ran with 0.5 only.
- The search was staged. A first grid (step 0.1, 0.2, 0.3 and weights 0.005, 0.01, 0.02 at 2000 iterations), then 3000 iterations at the best setting, then up to two extensions of the weight (0.05 and 0.1, then 0.2 and 0.5) that ran only while the best weight was the largest tried. The range was widened after the first stage.
- The collusion weight of 0.5 is the edge of the grid. Step 0.1 is the lowest step tried. 3000 iterations is the highest count tried, and 3000 was only tried with step 0.1. A stronger collusion attack than the one evaluated may exist.
- The aggregate view got 7 settings, the other two views 14 each, so the aggregate attack had the smaller search.
- The `max_weight` 16 rows were not retuned. They reuse the `max_weight` 1000 settings.

## Results (20 seeds, 160 victim images per row)

Recognisable rate with Wilson 95% interval, then mean MSE and the seed-level bootstrap interval for the gap to the floor (percentage points).

| View | Rate, Wilson (optimistic) | Rate, seed bootstrap | Mean MSE | Gap to floor, seed bootstrap |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Label floor (class average) | 58.1% [50.4, 65.5] | [51.2, 65.0] | 0.0521 | 0 |
| Mean image (for context) | 11.3% [7.2, 17.1] | [6.9, 15.6] | 0.0895 | |
| Plaintext individual update | 86.3% [80.1, 90.7] | [80.0, 91.9] | 0.0305 | +28.1 [+20.6, +35.6] |
| n-1 collusion, max_weight 1000 | 67.5% [59.9, 74.3] | [56.3, 77.5] | 0.0419 | +9.4 [+0.6, +18.8] |
| Aggregate only, max_weight 1000 | 45.0% [37.5, 52.7] | [36.3, 54.4] | 0.0598 | -13.1 [-22.5, -3.8] |
| n-1 collusion, max_weight 16 | 71.9% [64.5, 78.3] | [60.6, 81.9] | 0.0373 | +13.7 [+4.4, +23.1] |
| Aggregate only, max_weight 16 | 53.1% [45.4, 60.7] | [45.6, 61.3] | 0.0526 | -5.0 [-12.5, +2.5] |

The Wilson column is optimistic. It treats the 160 images as independent, but the 8 images of a seed share an initial model and a data draw. The seed bootstrap resamples the 20 seeds
(10,000 resamples, resampling seed 9100) and is the more honest interval. It is wider for every attack row (the two reference rows are slightly narrower). Both are recomputed from the per-seed files by `scripts/recompute_fashion_seed_bootstrap.py`
(output `fashion_mnist/seed_bootstrap.json`).

**X1 (primary, max_weight 1000): FAIL.**
- Plaintext at least floor + 10: yes, +28.1.
- n-1 collusion at least floor + 10: NO, +9.4.
- Aggregate within floor + 5: yes, -13.1.

Secondary check at max_weight 16, same rule: plaintext +28.1, collusion +13.7, aggregate -5.0. All three hold. It does not change the verdict.

What the numbers say. The ordering matches the digits study: plaintext (86%) above collusion (68%) above the label floor (58%) above the aggregate view (45%). The collusion miss is a margin of 0.6 points.
The seed bootstrap interval on the collusion gap is [+0.6, +18.8], and the share of resamples with a gap of at least 10 points is about 0.475 (0.478 with this resampling seed). X1 is close to a coin flip, and 20 seeds do not settle whether the true gap is above 10.

The quantiser plausibly accounts for about 4 of the points. At `max_weight` 1000 each client's weights are scaled by 8/1000 before quantisation. The paired difference between the `max_weight` 16 and `max_weight` 1000 collusion rates is
+4.4 points (seed bootstrap 95% [+1.3, +7.5]). This is a plausible explanation and was not tested further. The `max_weight` 16 row was not retuned, it reuses the `max_weight` 1000 attack settings.
Even at `max_weight` 16 collusion is about 14 points below plaintext (71.9% against 86.3%), so the quantiser is not the whole story.
In this observation model a finer weight ratio gives a colluding server a cleaner update. As with S1, the aggregate view being below the floor is a statement about this attack, not a privacy guarantee.

Plaintext recovery is lower than on digits (86% against 95%) and the class-average floor is lower too (58% against 75%). We did not test why. Image size, the CNN and the harder classes are all candidates.

## Limits

- One small CNN with ReLU and max pooling. Other architectures can be easier or harder to invert.
- Attack settings come from 3 tuning seeds (24 images), an MSE criterion that disagrees with the rate criterion for the collusion view, and a staged search that stopped at an edge for the collusion view (see the tuning notes above).
- 20 evaluation seeds locally. Intervals are wide for the collusion gap. The hosted workflow uses 30 by default and has not been run.
- Observation-model study with real quantiser arithmetic, not an attack on captured Docker traffic. One local step, known labels, known initial model.
- Wilson intervals are pooled over images and are optimistic. The seed bootstrap is shown for every row because images of one seed share an initial model.

## Reproduce and dispatch

```
python scripts/run_fashion_mnist_study.py --download --tune --workers 2   # about 15 minutes on 2 CPUs
python scripts/run_fashion_mnist_study.py --seeds 20 --workers 2          # about 35 minutes on 2 CPUs
python scripts/write_prereg_checks_wp9_wp10.py
python scripts/recompute_fashion_seed_bootstrap.py                        # seed bootstrap intervals, a few seconds
gh workflow run fashion-mnist-study.yml -f seeds=30                       # hosted, needs the workflow on main
pytest tests/training/test_fashion_mnist.py tests/integration/test_fashion_mnist_workflow_static.py
```

The hosted run writes to `reports/results/privacy/v0150/fashion_mnist/hosted/` and tunes again from scratch, so its chosen settings can differ from the local ones. It is a robustness check.
It cannot change the X1 verdict, which is the local 20-seed result at `max_weight` 1000. A hosted result is reported next to it as a second observation.
