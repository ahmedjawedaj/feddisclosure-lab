Pre-registration: `docs/preregistration/v0.15.md`. Thresholds were not changed. Results are in
`reports/results/privacy/v0150/fashion_mnist/` and the verdict in `reports/results/privacy/v0150/prereg_check_fashion.json`.

**X1 is recorded as FAIL.** Plaintext and the aggregate view meet their rules. n-1 collusion at the primary
setting reaches +9.4 points over the floor, 0.6 points short of the required +10. The same rule at a finer SecAgg+ weight ratio
passes. Both outcomes are reported below, the verdict stays FAIL.

## Model and protocol

Small CNN: two 3x3 convolutions (8 and 16 channels) with ReLU and max pooling, then one linear layer. 9,098 parameters.
The architecture was fixed before any tuning or evaluation run.

The protocol is the digits view study (`docs/v0.15-scaling.md`) on 28x28 images. 4 clients hold 8 distinct images each and take one full-batch SGD step
(learning rate 0.15) from a shared initial model. Client 0 is the victim. The attacker knows labels, learning rate and initial model.
Per-image MSE below 0.05 on pixels in [0, 1] is "recognisable". Views:

- plaintext: the victim update.
- n-1 collusion: the SecAgg+ aggregate (upstream quantiser, clipping range 8, quantisation range 2^22) minus the other clients' updates.
- aggregate only: the SecAgg+ aggregate with joint-oracle labels, matching all 32 images at once.
- label floor: every victim record rebuilt as the class-average image of the 59,968 public images that no client holds.

The attack is the repository's gradient matching, generalised to the CNN (`fashion_mnist_study.invert`). It sees only the initial model, the observed delta, the labels and the learning rate.
The primary SecAgg+ `max_weight` is 1000 (the upstream default and the value used for S1 and S2), fixed before the run. `max_weight` 16 is a secondary setting.

**Tuning.** Attack settings (iterations, step size, total-variation weight) were chosen on tuning seeds 9000 to 9002, separately for the individual,
collusion and aggregate views, by lowest mean victim MSE (`tuning.json`, 35 settings in total). Chosen: 3000 iterations and step 0.1 for all three,
total-variation weight 0.1 (plaintext), 0.5 (collusion) and 0.05 (aggregate). The collusion weight sits at the end of the search range
(the search was extended upward until the gain flattened: 0.0395 at 0.2 and 0.0382 at 0.5), so a stronger attacker might gain a little more.
Heavy smoothing is a prior, not information from the update, which is why every number is shown next to the label floor.

## Results (20 seeds, 160 victim images per row)

Recognisable rate with Wilson 95% interval, then mean MSE and the seed-level bootstrap interval for the gap to the floor (percentage points).

| View | Rate | Mean MSE | Gap to floor |
|---|---|---|---|
| Label floor (class average) | 58.1% [50.4, 65.5] | 0.0521 | 0 |
| Mean image (for context) | 11.3% [7.2, 17.1] | 0.0895 | |
| Plaintext individual update | 86.3% [80.1, 90.7] | 0.0305 | +28.1 [+20.6, +35.6] |
| n-1 collusion, max_weight 1000 | 67.5% [59.9, 74.3] | 0.0419 | +9.4 [+0.6, +18.8] |
| Aggregate only, max_weight 1000 | 45.0% [37.5, 52.7] | 0.0598 | -13.1 [-21.9, -3.7] |
| n-1 collusion, max_weight 16 | 71.9% [64.5, 78.3] | 0.0373 | +13.7 [+4.4, +23.1] |
| Aggregate only, max_weight 16 | 53.1% [45.4, 60.7] | 0.0526 | -5.0 [-12.5, +2.5] |

**X1 (primary, max_weight 1000): FAIL.**
- Plaintext at least floor + 10: yes, +28.1.
- n-1 collusion at least floor + 10: NO, +9.4.
- Aggregate within floor + 5: yes, -13.1.

Secondary check at max_weight 16, same rule: plaintext +28.1, collusion +13.7, aggregate -5.0. All three hold. It does not change the verdict.

What the numbers say. The ordering matches the digits study: plaintext (86%) above collusion (68%) above the label floor (58%) above the aggregate view (45%). The collusion miss is a margin of 0.6 points
and the interval on the gap ([+0.6, +18.8]) is wide, so 20 seeds do not settle whether the true gap is above 10. The likely cause of the smaller collusion margin is the quantiser,
not the attack. At `max_weight` 1000 each client's weights are scaled by 8/1000 before quantisation, and for this small CNN the quantisation error in the recovered victim update has a
root mean square of about 18% of the update itself (about 0.4 x 10^-3 against 2.2 x 10^-3, measured on one seed). At `max_weight` 16 that share is under 1%. This is a property of the protocol setting and of how small the updates are,
so a deployment with a finer weight ratio leaks more to a colluding server. As with S1, the aggregate view being below the floor is a statement about this attack, not a privacy guarantee.

Plaintext recovery is lower than on digits (86% against 95%) and the class-average floor is lower too (58% against 75%). We did not test why. Image size, the CNN and the harder classes are all candidates.

## Limits

- One small CNN with ReLU and max pooling. Other architectures can be easier or harder to invert.
- Attack settings come from 3 tuning seeds and a search that stopped at an edge for the collusion view.
- 20 evaluation seeds locally. Intervals are wide for the collusion gap. The hosted workflow uses 30 by default.
- Observation-model study with real quantiser arithmetic, not an attack on captured Docker traffic. One local step, known labels, known initial model.
- Wilson intervals are pooled over images. The seed bootstrap is shown for the gap because images of one seed share an initial model.

## Reproduce and dispatch

```
python scripts/run_fashion_mnist_study.py --download --tune --workers 2   # about 15 minutes on 2 CPUs
python scripts/run_fashion_mnist_study.py --seeds 20 --workers 2          # about 35 minutes on 2 CPUs
python scripts/write_prereg_checks_wp9_wp10.py
gh workflow run fashion-mnist-study.yml -f seeds=30                       # hosted, needs the workflow on main
pytest tests/training/test_fashion_mnist.py tests/integration/test_fashion_mnist_workflow_static.py
```

The hosted run writes to `reports/results/privacy/v0150/fashion_mnist/hosted/` and tunes again from scratch, so its chosen settings can differ from the local ones. It is an independent
check, not a replacement.
